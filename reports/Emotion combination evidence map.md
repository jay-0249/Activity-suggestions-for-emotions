# Shared-mechanism activities beat stacking single-emotion fixes

Negative emotions in this app's 12 classes come in predictable clusters, and the evidence now supports a combination engine built around them rather than around 66 independent pairs. Three clusters recur across experience-sampling, mood-scale and clinical data: a **tense-distress cluster** (stressed, anxious, angry, sad; DASS scale correlations .77–.83, within-person worried–sad r = .45), a **depleted pairing** (sad + tired; POMS r = .73 in a general-population sample, fatigue in about 93% of major depression), and a **disconnected pairing** (lonely + bored, which forms its own factor and correlates r ≈ .36–.57). Mixed-valence moments are normal too: a third of sampled moments contain both a positive and a negative emotion. On the intervention side, no trial recruited people because two momentary emotions were high together. Still, a substantial body of same-sample studies shows that a few activity families move several negative dimensions at once in the same people. These are nature and green walks, Hatha yoga, slow breathing or mindfulness, and self-paced movement. A handful of trials in mixed-state samples (high stress plus depressive symptoms; stressed and fatigued nurses; lonely, depressed and anxious homebound adults) improved both targeted states. The practical consequence is to lead a multi-emotion profile with one of these "shared-mechanism" activities and then add one component-specific step, while respecting the union of all avoid lists. The evidence confirms the direction of most existing combination rules (R10–R27). It refines most of them on dose and ordering. It contradicts two specifics: calming as the default for excited-plus-anxious before a performance, and undirected expressive journalling for angry-plus-sad. Almost all of this rests on multi-week programmes or 15–90 minute sessions, so a 5–10 minute in-app suggestion remains an extrapolation, and the honest copy is still "may help a bit right now".

## How to read the numbers and tags in this report

Each figure carries the verification tag from the research notes. **[PM-full]** means the number was read in the PubMed Central full text or a table fetched from it. **[PM-abs]** means it was read in the PubMed abstract. **[web]** means it came only from a search-engine summary or a secondary source, and **[memory]** means it is background knowledge not re-checked. Treat [web] and [memory] as unconfirmed (equivalent to "UNC" in the earlier emotion-to-activity report), quote them as "the cited claim", and check them before they reach a slide or UI string. A few rows carry **[earlier pass]**: these were confirmed against the abstract in the first research round and not re-fetched here.

Three caveats apply throughout. First, most co-occurrence numbers are **between-person scale correlations** (people who score high on one scale tend to score high on another). These overstate how often two feelings arise in the same moment. In one day-reconstruction study, worried–sad correlated .85 between people but only .45 within people ([Lucas et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6555425/)) [PM-full], and the within-person figure is the right prior for a momentary check-in. Second, construct mapping is approximate. POMS/BRUMS "tension" uses anxiety words and could map to anxious as easily as to stressed [memory]. PANAS positive affect is activated affect, closer to energetic than content. Almost no study measured calm, content, restless, bored or neutral directly. Third, the drawing check-in itself is not a validated measure of momentary emotion (see the earlier report). Every rule here should therefore key on the labels the user confirms, not on raw model scores.

Row-level data live in the companion dataset in `data/`: `pair_prevalence.csv` and `pair_prevalence_summary.csv` for co-occurrence, `combination_evidence.csv`/`.json` for intervention rows, `baseline_moderation.csv`, `combination_matrix.csv` for all 66 pairs, `combination_triples.csv`, `combination_rules.json` and `schema.md`.

## Negative feelings arrive in three clusters, not random pairs

Everyday data establish that blends are the norm. In smartphone experience sampling of more than 11,000 people, **33% of moments were mixed-valence**, 41% positive only and 16% negative only. Anxiety (29% of moments) and sadness (20%) were the commonest negative states, and anxiety was among the emotions that most often co-occurred with an opposite-valence emotion ([Trampe, Quoidbach & Taquet 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4689475/)) [PM-full]. In high-school students, **happy, tired and stressed** were among the most frequent emotions to occur alongside emotions of opposite valence ([Moeller et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29389203/)) [PM-abs]. A detector returning anxious + content, tired + content or stressed + content is therefore reporting a realistic state, not noise.

The structure of the negative states is clearest in a day-reconstruction study of adults aged 60 and over. Whole-day ratings split into three factors. One was positive. One was "negative arousal", with stressed .80, frustrated .78, worried .67, angry .66 and sad .58. The third was its own factor of **lonely .735 and bored .685**, with sad cross-loading .33 ([Lucas et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6555425/)) [PM-full]. Standard mood scales reinforce the first cluster. DASS-21 depression, anxiety and stress intercorrelate **.77–.83** in health professionals, an occupational group that probably runs above general-population levels ([Kakemam et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8789546/)) [PM-full]. Italian BRUMS tension, anger and depression intercorrelate .63–.65 ([Quartiroli et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5594224/)) [PM-full]. They also add the depleted pairing: in a representative German sample of 2,006, **dejection–fatigue r = .733** and dejection–anger .754 ([Petrowski et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8200627/)) [PM-full].

Clinical data identify which triples dominate when distress is severe. In a primary-care sample, **72.9% of people with major depression also had GAD** and 69.2% of GAD cases had depression. Among comorbid cases, **fatigue was endorsed by 94.2%**, irritability by 84.5% and psychomotor agitation by 93.7% ([Zbozinek et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3629816/)) [PM-full, derived percentages]. That makes sad + anxious + tired, sad + anxious + angry and anxious + restless the priority clinical triples. Overt irritability or anger accompanied **54.5%** of major depressive episodes ([Judd et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24026579/)) [PM-abs]. Loneliness sits next to this cluster. Pooled correlations are **.49 with depression and .40 with anxiety** across 73 studies ([Gabarrell-Pascuet et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9957328/)) [PM-full]. In a Korean national survey, **48.1% of highly lonely adults screened positive for both depression and anxiety, and 28.7% also for anger** ([Cho et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12258374/)) [PM-full, adjusted table values; the abstract gives conflicting single-outcome figures]. Boredom tracks low mood as closely as it tracks loneliness: boredom–depression r = .60 and boredom–loneliness .53 in 523 adults ([Hager et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13630348/)) [PM-full].

## Same-sample studies show which activities move several feelings at once

The question for a combination rule is not "does activity X help emotion A, and separately emotion B?" It is "did A and B both improve in the same people?" That evidence exists. It is unevenly spread, and it reveals that some dimensions move together while others do not.

### Nature and green walks: the broadest joint effect, with a dose split

Forest and park walks are the only activity with dozens of replications on all six POMS subscales in the same participants. In 585 young adults, a 15-minute forest walk beat a city walk on all five negative subscales and on vigor. The sizes differed sharply: post-walk tension-anxiety T-scores were 35.5 vs 40.4 and fatigue 37.5 vs 42.7, but anger-hostility only 38.1 vs 39.5 ([Song et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30544682/)) [PM-full]. A 2025 subscale meta-analysis shows that **dose decides which dimensions move**. Sessions of 60 minutes or more improved all six subscales (tension WMD −1.79, depression −1.42, anger −1.67, fatigue −2.13, vigor +2.00). In 15–60 minute sessions, tension, depression and anger fell slightly but **fatigue (−0.13, p = .68) and vigor (+0.09, p = .58) did not change**. European samples showed no fatigue or vigor effect at all ([Front Psychol 2025, PMID 41256089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41256089/)) [PM-full]. That review has internal table inconsistencies and publication bias on the tension scale (Egger p = .001). Under thermally neutral conditions, another meta-analysis found tension SMD −1.36, fatigue −0.90 and vigor +1.23, with inconsistent effects in heat or cold ([Sci Rep 2025, PMID 41214175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41214175/)) [PM-abs].

**Anger is the least responsive dimension to nature.** It was unaffected by two 80-minute forest walks ([Li et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27493670/)) [PM-full], not significant after a spring urban-park walk ([Song et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24887352/)) [PM-full], and showed no environment main effect in 498 volunteers ([Morita et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17055544/)) [PM-abs]. Depression also failed to move in two of the short urban-park studies. For a 10–20 minute walk, then, the reliable joint effect is **anxious/stressed + sad**, sometimes with tired. Rules that send angry users to nature should say the angry component is the weakest-supported part.

### Exercise lifts energy, not fatigue, and in low mood it ties with rest

Acute aerobic exercise raises energy reliably but does not lower fatigue. Across 16 studies the energy change was Δ = 0.47 while fatigue was Δ = 0.03. Fatigue fell only after more than 20 minutes at low-to-moderate intensity ([Loy, O'Connor & Dishman 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266)) [web; journal not in PubMed]. In persistently fatigued young adults, fatigue fell only after **low**-intensity sessions ([Dishman et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20409016/)) [PM-abs], and a maximal walking test *raised* fatigue and lowered vigor ([Physiol Behav 1995, PMID 7878100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7878100/)) [PM-abs]. The most important same-sample result for the depleted pairing comes from patients with major depression. Thirty minutes of treadmill exercise and 30 minutes of **quiet rest reduced tension, depression, anger and fatigue equally**, and only exercise raised vigor and wellbeing ([Bartholomew, Morrison & Ciccolo 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16331126/)) [PM-abs; n = 40 is memory]. A 2025 trial found the same for resistance exercise: depressed mood fell with exercise (d = 0.29–1.26) and with rest (d = 0.27–0.39), including in people with comorbid analogue GAD and depression, with no group difference ([MSSE 2025, PMID 40440519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440519/)) [PM-abs]. For sad + tired, rest is an evidence-based option in its own right, not a fallback. Movement is the pick when the goal is energy.

Programme-level physical activity improves the tense-distress cluster broadly: median effects of −0.43 for depression, −0.42 for anxiety and −0.60 for psychological distress across 97 reviews, though 77 of those reviews were rated critically low quality ([Singh et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36796860/)) [PM-abs]. A single bout moves state anxiety only slightly (g = 0.16) ([Ensari et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25899389/)) [PM-abs]. For acute anxiety in young people, mind-body exercise (SMD −0.36) outperformed aerobic (−0.16), and resistance exercise was not significant ([Scand J Med Sci Sports 2024, PMID 39317982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39317982/)) [PM-abs].

### Mind-body practices split by arousal, and PMR misses sadness

One 60-minute Hatha yoga session lowered tension, depression, anger, fatigue and confusion and raised vigor in 253 women ([Healthcare 2026, PMID 42121565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42121565/)) [PM-abs]. Single classes of yoga, Feldenkrais or swimming improved both state anxiety and depressive mood in 147 teachers, while **aerobic dance did not** ([Netz & Lidor 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14629072/)) [PM-abs]. A same-session comparison shows the arousal split cleanly. Yoga lowered perceived stress, negative affect and cortisol without raising positive affect. African dance lowered stress and negative affect *and* raised positive affect, while cortisol rose ([West et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15454358/)) [PM-abs]. Yoga suits stressed + anxious; dance suits stressed + sad or stressed + bored.

Brief breathing works on several dimensions at once. In a remote RCT, 5 minutes a day of breathwork or mindfulness lowered daily negative affect and state anxiety and raised positive affect. Cyclic sighing produced the largest positive-affect gain and a negative-affect change of −1.48 ± 1.69, and mindfulness lowered state anxiety −3.95 ± 4.16 ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)) [PM-full]. Pooled across separate trials, breathwork lowers stress (g = −0.35), anxiety (−0.32) and depressive symptoms (−0.40) ([Fincham et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36624160/)) [PM-abs]. Those are parallel estimates from different trials, not joint change. Brief mindfulness training reduced both anxiety and fatigue relative to an audiobook ([Zeidan et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20363650/)) [PM-abs]. Progressive muscle relaxation is the exception. In prostate-cancer patients it lowered DASS anxiety and stress, but **depression did not change (p = .956)** ([APJCP 2013, PMID 23725119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23725119/)) [PM-abs]. PMR is a stress/anxiety tool and a weak match whenever sad is elevated.

### Social, creative and play activities: mood moves easily, specific states do not

The creative and social literature has a consistent pattern: **global positive and negative affect move after almost any engaging activity, but specific states such as loneliness, worry and depression often do not move with them**. One 60-minute online dance class raised positive affect and lowered depressive symptoms, but **UCLA loneliness did not change** ([Humphries et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36647172/)) [PM-full]. Adaptive Tetris while waiting for uncertain news raised positive and lowered negative emotion, but **did not reduce worry** ([Rankin, Walsh & Sweeny 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30265082/)) [PM-abs]. Best-possible-self writing raises positive affect (d+ = 0.511) but not negative affect (d+ = 0.192, ns) or depression (d+ = 0.115, ns) ([Carrillo et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31545815/)) [PM-full]. Gratitude does not lower anxiety against an alternative activity (d = .11, ns) ([Davis et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26575348/)) [PM-abs]. These are "add positive" tools for a low-content or low-energy profile, not for anxious or sad combinations.

Only activities with a social channel and repeated contact show loneliness falling together with sadness or anxiety. Four weeks of empathy-focused phone calls lowered UCLA loneliness (d = 0.48), PHQ depression (d = 0.31) and GAD-7 anxiety (d = 0.35) in 240 homebound adults; a second loneliness scale was not significant (d = 0.17) ([Kahlon et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33620417/)) [PM-full]. Six months of community choir lowered loneliness (ES 0.34) and raised interest in life (ES 0.39), the only same-sample lonely + bored result found ([Johnson et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30412233/)) [PM-abs]. Ten weeks of group drumming lowered both depression (−3.41) and anxiety (−2.21) relative to control, while perceived stress fell in both arms ([Fancourt et al. 2016, PLoS One](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26974430/)) [PM-full]. A 20-minute dog interaction cut state anxiety by about 13 points versus 2 with a handler alone and raised wellbeing (anxiety η² = .30) ([Grajfoner et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28475132/)) [PM-full]. A therapy-dog drop-in lowered stress and raised happiness and energy at once ([Ward-Griffin et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29528189/)) [PM-abs], a rare stressed + content + energetic result. That supports interacting with an existing or available animal, not acquiring one: pet ownership shows no association with depression (OR 1.03, ns) ([Moshfeghinia et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41194144/)) [PM-abs].

Single-session humour shows a narrow joint effect. A comedy video lowered POMS anger-hostility and raised vigor in 10 women, while a tragedy video raised depression ([Sakuragi et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12148458/)) [PM-abs]. Laughter programmes lower depression and anxiety together across 10 RCTs ([Zhao et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30882915/)) [PM-abs]. But watching entertainment TV matched laughter therapy on both in a small pilot ([Ko & Park 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37450484/)) [PM-full], so much of this benefit looks non-specific. Music has a direction effect for sadness: in a choir, **singing lowered negative affect while listening to choral music raised it** ([Kreutz et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15669447/)) [PM-abs], and people with depression are more prone to "group rumination" with music ([Garrido, Eerola & McFerran 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00490)) [PM-abs]. Active or shared music is therefore a better fit than solitary sad-music loops for sad + lonely.

## Mixed-state trials and baseline load point the same way: benefit is largest where scores are high

### Trials in people with two elevated states

A small set of trials enrolled people defined by two high states, and each improved both. **Stressed + sad has the most direct evidence.** In 84 midlife women with high stress and high depressive symptoms, three weeks of either Wim Hof breathing with cold showers or slow breathing with warm showers cut depression by 24%, anxiety by 27% and stress by 20%, with gains maintained at three months ([Compr Psychoneuroendocrinol 2024, PMID 39606690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39606690/)) [PM-abs]. In 141 women with high perceived stress, mindfulness, slow breathing, Wim Hof and HIIT all reduced depressive symptoms (−15.9%) and stress (−9.7%) with no difference between arms ([Ann Behav Med 2025, PMID 41309213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41309213/)) [PM-abs]. Because the gentle arms matched the hormetic ones, slow breathing is as good a first suggestion and easier to tolerate. For **stressed + tired**, 20-minute sessions of progressive muscle relaxation with music over eight weeks lowered both stress and fatigue in ICU nurses compared with an attention control ([Ozgundondu & Gok Metin 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31371164/)) [PM-abs]. For **sad + tired**, forest bathing lowered depression, POMS negative mood and subjective fatigue relative to a city walk in 31 women with depression or depressive tendencies ([Li et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40277811/)) [PM-abs]. For **lonely + sad + anxious**, the Kahlon phone-call trial is the anchor. For **anxious + lonely**, four weeks of kind acts raised positive affect and lowered social-avoidance goals in 142 highly socially anxious adults, while anxiety-reduction exercises did not raise positive affect ([Alden & Trew 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22642341/)) [PM-abs]. In a dismantling RCT, mindfulness with acceptance training lowered daily loneliness (d = 0.44) and added about two social interactions a day (d = 0.47), whereas monitoring alone did not ([Lindsay et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30808743/)) [PM-abs].

Two experiments induced a blend and tested a response. When anxiety comes with high arousal before a performance, saying "I am excited" raised felt excitement and improved performance compared with trying to calm down, across karaoke, public-speaking and maths tasks ([Brooks 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/)) [PM-abs]. A field RCT of 339 community-college students found that stress-arousal reappraisal before an exam lowered evaluation anxiety and threat and raised scores ([Jamieson et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34292050/)) [PM-abs]. Across 44 effects, the pooled performance gain is small (d = 0.23), rising to 0.45 for combined interventions and 0.34 for public performance ([Bosshard & Gomez 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38575696/)) [PM-abs]. The other experiment is a caution. In 52 women, 30 minutes of moderate cycling **while viewing aversive images did not lower anxiety and increased anger** (time × group p = .028), with no such effect under neutral images ([Rodrigues et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41875642/)) [PM-abs]. Movement for angry or anxious blends must happen away from the provoking input.

The angry + sad blend has no combination trial, but each component's best tool has been tested in the relevant state. Distraction beat rumination: provoked people who ruminated for 25 minutes were more aggressive later, and provocation-related negative affect predicted aggression only among ruminators ([Bushman et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982116/)) [PM-abs]. Self-distanced "why" reflection let people revisit an anger memory without reactivating hot affect ([Kross, Ayduk & Mischel 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16137257/)) [PM-abs]. In adults with major depression, it lowered negative affect and depressive thought accessibility without avoidance ([Kross et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22708885/)) [PM-abs]. In children recalling a disappointment, drawing to distract improved mood more than drawing to vent ([Drake & Winner 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22963448/)) [PM-abs].

### Positive components in a blend do regulatory work

When a positive class co-occurs with a negative one, the evidence argues for keeping it. After an anxiety induction, contentment and amusement films produced faster cardiovascular recovery than neutral or sad films ([Fredrickson et al. 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21731120/)) [PM-abs]. Over a decade of experience sampling, frequent mixed emotions predicted better physical health ([Hershfield et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24032072/)) [PM-abs]. In 47 psychotherapy clients, happiness and sadness together preceded wellbeing gains ([Adler & Hershfield 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22539987/)) [PM-abs]. Habitual acceptance of mental experiences predicted lower negative affect under stress and better health six months later ([Ford et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28703602/)) [PM-abs]. All of this is correlational or longitudinal, so "both can be true" framing is supported as a stance, not as a tested intervention. Asking users to name each feeling separately has only weak support: emotion differentiation's links with psychopathology were "negligible to small" ([Eckland et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34766792/)) [PM-abs].

### Baseline moderation: weight by score, but expect floors and one exception

Several activities help more when the starting state is worse. Forest bathing's acute effect grew with chronic stress level ([Morita et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17055544/)) [PM-abs]. Workers with depressive tendencies started higher on every negative POMS subscale and fell to the level of their peers ([Furuyashiki et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31228960/)) [PM-full]. High trait anxiety predicted a larger drop in depression-dejection after a 15-minute forest walk ([Song et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30544682/)) [PM-full]. One hour of choir singing produced the largest stress reduction in those with the highest baseline stress (**r = −.789**), and stress fell more in those with higher anxiety and depression ([Fancourt et al. 2016, ecancer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27170831/)) [PM-full]. Floor effects are the mirror image. PMR did not move anxiety or depression in workers whose baseline levels were minimal ([Ind Health 2016, PMID 26726829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26726829/)) [PM-abs]. Awe walks did not move anxiety or depression in older adults starting near zero ([Sturm et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955293/)) [PM-full]. Choir did nothing for loneliness in students whose loneliness "was not elevated at baseline" ([Han et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40426352/)) [PM-full].

Most of these are pre-post designs, so part of "more benefit when worse" is regression to the mean, which Furuyashiki acknowledges. And the pattern has exceptions. Resistance training reduced anxiety more in healthy people (Δ 0.50) than in those with physical or mental illness (Δ 0.19) ([Gordon et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28819746/)) [PM-abs]. Kindness helped only altruistic adolescents ([Tashjian et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33278014/)) [PM-abs]. Nostalgia splits by design. Experiments find that it restores perceived support, particularly in resilient people ([Zhou et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19000213/)) [PM-abs] and at high loneliness ([Abeyta et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32655445/)) [PM-abs]. But five 14-day diary studies (N = 504) found daily nostalgia went with lower positive and higher negative affect, **more so on lonely days** ([Newman & Sachs 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32982886/)) [PM-abs]. Reminiscing about good times that cannot be repeated produced especially intense sadness ([Larsen et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34591508/)) [PM-abs]. The defensible engineering reading is to scale an activity's expected benefit with the elevated scores it targets, credit nothing for dimensions that are already low, and keep nostalgia as an opt-in, connection-framed option rather than a default for lonely + sad.

Two cross-cutting points follow from the table. The pairs with the most direct evidence (stressed + sad, stressed + tired, anxious + lonely, lonely + sad + anxious, energetic + anxious) are not the ones the original rule set marked as best supported. And the strongest "avoid" findings are cheaper to implement and better supported than most positive recommendations: no exercising amid the trigger, no venting or undirected writing, no maximal exercise sold as fatigue relief, and no PMR or gratitude as the only answer to low mood.

## Decision logic for a multi-emotion profile

The research licenses a specific procedure, though no study tested any ordering for two simultaneous emotions. The "regulate the most intense or highest-arousal emotion first" rule (R01) remains an extrapolation from lab choice data. Large daily-life datasets give **limited support** for the idea that people fit strategies to intensity or controllability ([Tran et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956182/)) [PM-abs]. The steps below are therefore a synthesis of the findings above, not a tested algorithm.

| Step | What the app does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Safety gate | Run R30 on the user's own words and confirmed labels. Increase sensitivity for lonely + sad + anxious and for bored + sad that persists across sessions | These triples mark the high-load groups in Cho 2025 and Eckland 2022. No validated threshold exists, so this is a precaution |
| 1. Confirm labels | Show the top two or three classes and let the user correct them | The drawing is not a validated measure; rules must key on confirmed labels |
| 2. Plausibility check | Accept common pairs silently. For antagonistic pairs (energetic + tired, calm + angry), ask once which fits better | Within-person and scale correlations above |
| 3. Map to a cluster | Tense distress, depleted, disconnected, positive, or a mixed-valence blend. Use a pair or triple rule if one exists, otherwise the dominant emotion's rule | The three-cluster structure in Lucas 2018 and the clinical triples |
| 4. Order | If angry is high, lower arousal and distract first. If tension and tiredness co-occur, choose low-energy-cost calming. If a performance is imminent with anxious + energetic, offer reappraisal first. Otherwise lead with a shared-mechanism activity | Anger evidence is the best tested; mixed-state trials favour gentle arms; Brooks/Jamieson |
| 5. Select | Score each candidate as the sum, over elevated classes, of its evidence weight times that class's score. Credit nothing for classes below threshold. Exclude anything on the avoid list of *any* elevated class | Baseline moderation and floor effects; the avoid lists carry the strongest evidence |
| 6. Offer two or three | One shared-mechanism activity plus one component-specific step (pleasant for sad, contact for lonely, challenge for bored, rest for tired), ranked by preferences within the allowed set | "More of the right strategies" predicts better mood; aversive-cognitive and disengagement strategies predict worse ([Southward & Cheavens 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32696710/)) [PM-abs] |
| 7. Honest copy | "May help a bit right now". For loneliness: "one message is a start; connection builds with repeat contact". For fatigue: "a short walk tends to lift energy; rest helps too" | Single sessions are small; loneliness did not move after one class; fatigue does not fall after short bouts |
| 8. Learn | Ask "did that help?" and re-rank per user | Polyregulation lowered distress only when most strategies were personally effective ([Liu et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42756577/)) [PM-abs] |

Several cautions apply to every profile. **Dose:** the multi-dimension nature effect that includes fatigue and vigor needed 60 minutes or more, and 15–60 minute sessions moved only tension, depression and anger. A 10-minute in-app walk should promise calmer, not less tired. **Specificity:** much single-session benefit looks non-specific (entertainment TV matched laughter therapy; quiet rest matched exercise in depression). The app should not claim one activity fixes a named emotion. **Context:** exercising while still exposed to the trigger raised anger, so movement prompts for any angry or anxious blend should say "step away from the screen first". **Population:** the mixed-state trials were in midlife women, nurses, homebound adults, socially anxious adults and depressed patients. None was in students using a phone for five minutes, so transfer to the app's users is an assumption.

## Conclusion

This round turns combination logic from pure composition into partly evidenced design, and it changes what the engine should optimise. The earlier framing treated a profile as a stack of single emotions, each needing its own fix. The same-sample data say that a small set of shared-mechanism activities (slow breathing, green walking, yoga, self-paced movement, warm one-to-one contact) move several of the most common co-occurring feelings at once, while specific states such as loneliness, worry and fatigue resist the generic mood lift that most activities deliver. That makes the most valuable engineering moves cheap ones. Lead with one shared-mechanism activity, add one targeted step, honour every avoid list in the profile, and scale expected benefit with the elevated scores. Keep the positive part of a mixed state rather than treating it as noise.

The evidence also exposes where the app can contribute. No study has tested ordering for simultaneous emotions, momentary co-occurrence rates for restless, bored, lonely, tired and neutral are missing, and every effective dose in the literature is longer than the app's window. Paired check-in labels, chosen activities and "did that help?" responses would generate exactly the within-person, multi-emotion, short-dose data the field lacks. That is the strongest case for logging them with consent from the first release.
