## 1. Graphology: can handwriting features reveal personality or psychological state?

### Takeaway
Graphology (reading character or disposition from slant, size, loops, etc.) is **pseudoscience**: every systematic review located finds validity at or near zero, and graphologists do no better than laypeople. The app must not use, or prompt an LLM to use, graphological rules ("large letters = extraversion", "heavy pressure = anger").

### Cited Findings
- Neter & Ben-Shakhar (1989) meta-analysed 17 studies: 63 graphologists and 51 non-graphologists rated 1,223 scripts; graphologists were no better than non-graphologists at predicting job performance. [V-abs; not in PubMed] — [Hebrew University research record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-predictive-validity-of-graphological-inferences-a-meta-analyt/); DOI [MEM, still unconfirmed] 10.1016/0191-8869(89)90021-3
- Dean (1992) reviewed roughly 200 studies and found only a small effect size for inferring personality from handwriting, with mean inter-rater agreement among graphologists of about r = .42; no school of graphology outperformed another. [V-sec, via King & Koehler and Wikipedia-derived summaries] — [Wikipedia: Graphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphology). Original: Dean, G. A. (1992), "The bottom line: effect size", in Beyerstein & Beyerstein (Eds.), *The Write Stuff*, Prometheus Books (book chapter, no DOI). Exact effect-size values were NOT retrieved — do not quote a number.
- King & Koehler (2000) showed experimentally that illusory correlation (semantic association between handwriting features and trait words) explains why people keep believing in graphology despite null evidence. [PM-abs: PMID 11218342, *J Exp Psychol Appl* 6(4):336–348; the abstract confirms that "semantic association between words used to describe handwriting features and personality traits was the source of biases in perceived correlation"] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11218342/); DOI confirmed (PubMed prints it as 10.1037//1076-898x.6.4.336)
- Dazzi & Pedrabissi (2009), *Psychological Reports* 105(3 Pt 2), 1255–1268: two studies (N = 101 with the Big Five Questionnaire; N = 102 autobiographical texts rated by two graphologists and two laypersons); "no evidence was found to validate the graphological method as a measure of personality." [PM-abs: PMID 20229925; in Study 1 two graphologists rated the samples and inter-rater reliability was "very low"] — [SAGE record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/PR0.105.F.1255-1268) (DOI 10.2466/PR0.105.F.1255-1268)
- British Psychological Society (1993), *Graphology in Personnel Assessment*: described in a *Nature* commentary as "an unremittingly negative picture"; secondary sources say the BPS ranked graphology alongside astrology at "zero validity". The exact "zero validity" wording is [V-sec] only. BPS hosts a guideline page with the same title. — [BPS guideline page](https://bps.org.uk/guideline/validity-graphology-personnel-assessment); secondary: [PsyBlog](https://www.spring.org.uk/2023/01/graphology-handwriting-analysis.php)
- Beyerstein & Beyerstein (1992), *The Write Stuff: Evaluations of Graphology — The Study of Handwriting Analysis* (Prometheus) is the edited volume containing Dean's meta-analysis. [V-sec] — [Wikipedia: Graphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphology)

### Inferences
- Graphology concerns stable traits; the app concerns momentary state. The null results do not strictly rule out state effects on handwriting *kinematics* (section 2), but they do rule out the "read meaning from the shape of the letters" approach, which is exactly what a vision LLM looking at a static image will tend to reproduce, because graphology folklore is abundant in training text.
- King & Koehler's illusory-correlation result is directly relevant to LLM output: a fluent, plausible-sounding reading ("jagged strokes suggest tension") will feel valid to users and judges regardless of whether it is.

### Gaps
- Dean's exact effect sizes and the BPS report's exact wording were not opened. Neither is needed to support the verdict, but neither should be quoted numerically.

## 2. Online (digitised) handwriting and drawing kinematics as markers of emotional state

### Takeaway
**Promising-but-preliminary, and weaker than headline accuracies suggest.** Pen-tablet kinematics (timing, in-air time, velocity, pressure) show group-level differences with depression, anxiety and stress questionnaire scores, with honest baseline accuracy around 60–71% on a 129-person dataset; higher figures (up to "100%") come from repeated re-analysis of that same small dataset. None of this was done with fingers on phone touchscreens, with 5–30 s free drawings, or with momentary (within-person) emotion.

### Cited Findings
- **EMOTHAW** (Likforman-Sulem, Esposito, Faundez-Zanuy, Clémençon, Cordasco; *IEEE Trans. Human-Machine Systems* 47(2), 2017): 129 participants; emotional states (anxiety, depression, stress) "assessed by the Depression-Anxiety-Stress Scales (DASS) questionnaire"; seven tasks recorded on a digitizing tablet — pentagons and house drawing, words copied in handprint, circles and clock drawing, one sentence copied in cursive; records pen positions on-paper and in-air, time stamp, pressure, pen azimuth and altitude. [V-abs] — [IP Paris research portal](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/emothaw-a-novel-database-for-emotional-state-recognition-from-han/); preprint of the same paper [arXiv:2202.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245) [arXiv: abstract opened; author order and all details in this bullet confirmed]; journal DOI 10.1109/THMS.2016.2635441 [arXiv: the arXiv record lists this DOI; the paper is not in PubMed]
- EMOTHAW baseline result: random-forest classifiers with cross-validation; "the targeted emotional states can be identified with accuracies ranging from 60% to 71%" [arXiv: confirmed verbatim]; CHECKED [IEEE journal full text, Table III]: depression accuracy 71.2% but sensitivity only 12.6% (specificity 92.2%); anxiety 60% (sensitivity 45%, specificity 71%); stress 60.2% (sensitivity 41%, specificity 74.5%); 10 repetitions of leave-one-out cross-validation. The authors note that high accuracy can come from always predicting the majority class, and that in ROC space "anxiety and stress recognition ... perform better than depression recognition"; the depression models "have the best overall accuracies" but "are the worst in ROC space". Labels are DASS-42 scores for the past week. A clock drawing took 8.7 s for a non-depressed participant vs 36.3 s for a depressed one — [arXiv:2202.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245)
- Cordasco et al. (2019), "Handwriting and drawing features for detecting negative moods": DASS-42-defined depressed, stressed and anxious subjects compared with age- and gender-matched controls; significant differences from controls depending on exercise and feature category (time vs. frequency of events); describes a feature database from 240 subjects. [V-abs; not in PubMed; effect sizes not retrieved] — [University of Salerno record](https://www.iris.unisa.it/handle/11386/4906497). A later clinical study from the same group is in PubMed: Greco et al. (2024), *Int J Neural Syst* 34(2):2350069, PMID 38009869, DOI 10.1142/S0129065723500697 — depressed patients vs. healthy controls on seven tablet tasks; all feature categories except pressure discriminated the groups [PM-abs; group sizes not shown in the abstract]
- Nolazco-Flores, Faundez-Zanuy, et al. (2021), *IEEE Access*, "Emotional State Recognition Performance Improvement on a Handwriting and Drawing Task": same EMOTHAW data; time, spectral and cepstral features; RBF-SVM with leave-one-out; reports "accuracy classification improvement ranging from 4% to 34%" over baseline. [V-abs; not in PubMed] — [author-repository PDF](https://iris.unicampania.it/retrieve/dfd1c04d-8960-0799-e053-6605fe0a8ddb/IEEEAccess2021.pdf)
- Nolazco-Flores & Faundez-Zanuy et al. (2022), *Sensors* 22(4):1686, "Mood State Detection in Handwritten Tasks Using PCA–mFCBF and Automated Machine Learning": 82.5% / 72.8% / 74.56% for three-level depression / anxiety / stress; "100% accuracy" for two-level severity; training data were augmented by adding Gaussian noise to features; authors note participants share a similar demographic background. [PM-full: PMID 35214585; accuracies and augmentation confirmed in the abstract; the full text states EMOTHAW has 129 participants (71 female, 58 male) aged 21–32, all right-handed students of Università degli Studi della Campania L. Vanvitelli, Italy, and that augmentation used Gaussian noise scaled by 0.2] — [PMC8875261](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8875261/) (DOI 10.3390/s22041686). Author list per PubMed: Nolazco-Flores, Faundez-Zanuy, Velázquez-Flores, Del-Valle-Soto, Cordasco, Esposito
- Rahman & Halim (2023, *Applied Intelligence*; note: 2023, not 2022), "Identifying dominant emotional state using handwriting and drawing samples by fusing features": temporal, spectral and MFCC features with a BiLSTM on writing/drawing signals; reports fusion "improve[s] recognition accuracy significantly". Specific accuracy NOT retrieved. [V-sec; not in PubMed] — [catalogue record](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c319t-8c413cd4e8c9125d8128196625a70d63f616792de3ec28c7c980bf27ff805ec13)
- Khan, Xia, Aurangzeb, Khaliq, Alam, Khan & Anwar (2024), *PeerJ Computer Science* 10:e1887, apply an attention-based transformer to handwriting/drawing emotion detection and report 92.64% on EMOTHAW (the abstract does not say which task or split). [PM-abs: PMID 38660197, PMC11041987, DOI 10.7717/peerj-cs.1887] — [PeerJ cs-1887](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-1887)
- Bay Ayzeren, Erbilek & Çelebi (2019), *IEEE Access* 7, 164759–164774: 134 participants, 804 handwriting and 8,040 signature samples, labelled happy / sad / stress, both online and offline; abstract claims "remarkable success especially on stress prediction for handwriting". Numeric accuracies and the emotion-induction procedure were NOT retrieved. [V-abs; not in PubMed] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/24a3ff7a1dff4d89b6a1dc777db52f8d) (DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2952313)
- Same group: around 77% reported for stress prediction from *offline* signatures with deep learning (IWBF 2020). [V-sec] — [METU record](https://avesis.ncc.metu.edu.tr/yayin/7e04dad5-6f73-4188-832e-a257562d3102/deep-learning-based-stress-prediction-from-offline-signatures)
- Mergl et al. (2004), *Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica* 109, 383–391: 37 depressed patients vs. 37 healthy controls on a digitizing tablet; patients' drawing was less regular in velocity (P < 0.001) but of normal velocity, and handwriting was abnormally slow (P = 0.04). [PM-abs: PMID 15049774; Mergl, Juckel, Rihl, Henkel, Karner, Tigges, Schröter & Hegerl; *Acta Psychiatr Scand* 109(5):383–391] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15049774/); DOI 10.1046/j.1600-0447.2003.00262.x (from PubMed); earlier [citing record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi.dedup.....4fa04655d94540361c15837486fba8b2).
- Rosenblum, Werner, Dekel, Gurevitz & Heinik (2010), *Aging Clinical and Experimental Research* 22(2):141–147 (author list corrected: Werner is second author): 20 elderly people with mild major depressive disorder vs. 20 matched controls; the groups differed on the pressure measure in all four writing tasks, and pressure plus temporal and spatial measures of name-writing classified 84.2% correctly (a "preliminary study") [PM-abs: PMID 20440100, DOI 10.1007/BF03324787]; companion clock-drawing kinematic study by Heinik, Werner, Dekel, Gurevitz & Rosenblum (2010) in *International Psychogeriatrics* 22(3):479–488 (first author corrected from Werner to Heinik): same 20 vs. 20 design; pressure, azimuth and spatial measures were lower in the depressed group, performance time did not differ, 81.1% classified correctly (an "exploratory study") [PM-abs: PMID 19943993, DOI 10.1017/S1041610209991360] — [Cambridge record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-psychogeriatrics/article/computerized-kinematic-analysis-of-the-clock-drawing-task-in-elderly-people-with-mild-major-depressive-disorder-an-exploratory-study/D8AF82818B5407F0D7D4734F726E5018)
- Features repeatedly reported as carrying signal: time-based features (including in-air / pen-up time), ductus (stroke counts/order) and pressure for depression; on-paper vs. in-air features computed separately. [V-sec] — [PMC8875261](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8875261/); [arXiv:2202.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245)
- Mental workload (not emotion): 56 participants wrote on a digitizer under varying cognitive load; temporal and spatial/angular-velocity measures changed with load, **pressure measures did not**. [PM-abs: Luria & Rosenblum (2012), *Behav Res Methods* 44(2):575–586, PMID 21994184; "Fifty-six participants" and the pressure null confirmed] — [Behavior Research Methods, DOI 10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The labels are not momentary emotion.** DASS asks about symptoms over the past week, so EMOTHAW-family models classify *people* by recent symptom level (between-person), not the feeling a user has in this minute. The app's claim ("how you feel right now") is a different, untested construct. (DASS time frame is [MEM]: Lovibond & Lovibond 1995, DOI 10.1016/0005-7967(94)00075-U — citation and DOI [PM-abs: PMID 7726811]; the past-week time frame is not stated in the abstract and is not confirmed.)
- **Hardware mismatch.** All of this used a pen on a digitizing tablet with true pressure, pen tilt and in-air tracking. A finger on a phone gives no in-air trajectory, no tilt and (usually) no real pressure (see section 7). The best-supported feature family the app can still capture is *timing*: stroke durations, pauses between strokes, speed and its variability.
- **Task mismatch.** Evidence is for standardised copy tasks (pentagons, clock, a fixed sentence) so that people can be compared on identical content. Free drawing for 5–30 s confounds state with what the person chose to draw.
- **Inflation risk.** With N = 129 and hundreds of candidate features, feature selection plus leave-one-out, noise augmentation, and many papers tuning on the same dataset all push reported accuracy upward. "100%" on a two-class split of ~129 students should be treated as a red flag, not a benchmark. No independent-sample replication was located.
- Honest summary line for judges: "In lab pen-tablet studies, movement timing distinguishes higher from lower anxiety/stress questionnaire scorers about 60–70% of the time (chance ~50% for binary splits) — a real but small signal, never shown on phones or for moment-to-moment mood."

### Gaps
- Ayzeren et al.'s accuracy numbers and whether emotions were induced (e.g., by video) or self-reported: not retrieved.
- No study located using a Trier Social Stress Test or cortisol-verified stress with handwriting kinematics.
- No study located validating finger-on-touchscreen drawing dynamics against momentary affect.
- "Gupta et al." (named in the brief) could not be identified to a specific paper.
- EMOTHAW participant demographics: resolved in the PubMed pass — per Nolazco-Flores et al. 2022 (PMC full text), 129 right-handed students (71 female, 58 male, aged 21–32) of Università degli Studi della Campania L. Vanvitelli, Italy [PM-full].

## 3. Offline (static image) deep learning, and general-purpose vision LLMs

### Takeaway
**Weak.** Static-image results are sparse and unreplicated, and static images discard exactly the features (timing, in-air, pressure) that carried the signal in section 2. **No peer-reviewed evidence was found that general-purpose vision LLMs (GPT-4V/4o, Gemini, Claude) can validly infer a person's emotional state from handwriting or a free drawing.** The LLM papers that exist are preprints that measure agreement with expert *interpretations*, not accuracy against the drawer's actual state.

### Cited Findings
- Offline signatures, deep learning: about 77% stress-prediction accuracy reported by one group (IWBF 2020). [V-sec] — [METU record](https://avesis.ncc.metu.edu.tr/yayin/7e04dad5-6f73-4188-832e-a257562d3102/deep-learning-based-stress-prediction-from-offline-signatures)
- **Preprint** (arXiv 2512.21360, Dec 2025), multi-agent multimodal-LLM framework for House-Tree-Person drawings: mean semantic similarity between LLM interpretations and human expert interpretations of about 0.75. This is text similarity to experts, not validity against a mental-state criterion. [arXiv: abstract opened; Wen, Sun, Ku, Gao, Ma, Yang & Jiao, submitted 23 Dec 2025; "approximately 0.75 (standard deviation about 0.05)", rising to 0.85 in "structurally oriented expert data sets"] — [arXiv:2512.21360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21360v1)
- **Preprint** (arXiv 2510.19451, Oct 2025), "Reasoning Like Experts: Leveraging Multimodal LLMs for Drawing-based Psychoanalysis" (PICK): the emotion distributions used to train its reward model were themselves generated with GPT-4o from knowledge-base triplets. [arXiv: abstract and HTML full text opened; Ma, Jiang, Erfani, Bailey, Liu, Ehinger & Lau, submitted 22 Oct 2025, comment line "Accepted by ACM Multimedia 2025"; the full text says "we then use GPT-4o to generate an emotion distribution, categorizing each triplet into positive and negative proportions"] — [arXiv:2510.19451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19451); earlier secondary [review page](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/reasoning-like-experts-leveraging-multimodal-large-language-models-for-drawing-based-psychoanalysis)
- **Preprint** (arXiv 2601.04297, Jan 2026), "ArtCognition": combines image features with kinematic cues from the drawing process (stroke speed, pauses) plus retrieval-augmented generation; authors report "significant correlations" with standardised psychological metrics. Sample size and effect sizes not retrieved (not in the abstract). [arXiv: abstract opened; Binaei-Haghighi, Sajadi, Liviyan, Kharazi, Amirkhani & Bahrak, submitted 7 Jan 2026; the framework analyses the House-Tree-Person test] — [arXiv:2601.04297](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04297v1)
- Cautionary parallel from a far better-studied channel: Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez & Pollak (2019) concluded that facial movements "are not universally diagnostic of emotional states" and that software "does not detect the psychological meaning" of the movements it measures. [V-sec for the quotes; citation PM-abs: PMID 31313636, *Psychol Sci Public Interest* 20(1):1–68] — [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html); DOI 10.1177/1529100619832930 (confirmed in PubMed)

### Inferences
- A vision LLM returning `{"stress": 0.7, "anxiety": 0.4}` from a doodle is producing an *interpretation in the style of human interpreters*. Since the human interpreters' own methods (graphology, projective drawing signs) lack validity (sections 1 and 4), agreement with them is not evidence of accuracy.
- Decimal scores imply measurement precision that does not exist. This is the single least defensible element of the current design.
- What an LLM can defensibly do: describe visible properties (colour, density, line quality, content) and relate them to *documented population-level expressive conventions* (section 5), phrased as a prompt for the user's own reflection.

### Gaps
- No benchmark found that scores Gemini / Claude / GPT-4-class models on drawer-reported emotion from drawings or handwriting. Absence of evidence was established by one search pass only; a dedicated search may find more.
- Test-retest and cross-model consistency of LLM emotion scores for the same drawing: no published data found.

## 4. Projective drawing tests (House-Tree-Person, Draw-A-Person)

### Takeaway
**Weak (sign-based interpretation is close to pseudoscience).** The authoritative review found validity evidence for human figure drawings even more limited than for the Rorschach and TAT, with little incremental validity and a file-drawer problem. Recent deep-learning HTP papers reporting 89–99% depression-detection accuracy are small, single-site and internally validated; the one large study (N = 4,196; Lin et al. 2022) found the network **could not** separate depressed from non-depressed drawers.

### Cited Findings
- Lilienfeld, Wood & Garb (2000), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 1(2): only a small number of Rorschach/TAT indexes are empirically supported; "the validity evidence for human figure drawings is even more limited"; projective indexes "have not consistently demonstrated incremental validity above and beyond other psychometric data"; published effect sizes markedly exceeded unpublished ones (file-drawer effect). [PM-abs: PMID 26151980, pp. 27–66; every quoted phrase confirmed in the PubMed abstract] — [author-hosted PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~ghaeffel/Lilienfeld(2000).pdf); DOI 10.1111/1529-1006.002 (confirmed in PubMed)
- Motta, Little & Tobin (1993), "The use and abuse of human figure drawings", *School Psychology Quarterly* 8(3), 162–169: critical review concluding figure drawings should not be used as measures of personality/emotional adjustment. NOT retrieved this session — citation and conclusion are [MEM]; DOI [MEM] 10.1037/h0088271. [not in PubMed; still unverified]
- Large null result: Lin, Zhang, Qu, Li, Liu & Song (2022), "The House-Tree-Person test is not valid for the prediction of mental health: An empirical study using deep neural networks", *Acta Psychologica* 230:103734 (Oct 2022), 4,196 Chinese children and adolescents (1,890 female) — deep networks learned to recognise houses, trees and persons, but "failed to classify the drawings of depressive individuals from those of non-depressive individuals"; authors call the test's validity for diagnosing mental health problems doubtful; their review of earlier HTP studies also "found no reliable association between the existing HTP indicators and mental health problems studied". [PM-abs: PMID 36058187, DOI 10.1016/j.actpsy.2022.103734] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a1d067ec7ddd43ab98d127d84e4ee9e7)
- High-accuracy claims: FBANet transfer-learning model, *Entropy* 25(9):1350 (2023) — maximum 99.07% validation accuracy, 97.71% mean in cross-validation, with authors acknowledging "the limited size of the collected data". [PM-abs: Wang, Zhang, Huang & Cai; PMID 37761649, PMC10529103, DOI 10.3390/e25091350; dataset size is not given in the abstract] — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/1099-4300/25/9/1350)
- 2024 feasibility study (Zhang, Yu, Barra, Ruan, Chen & Cai, *Comput Methods Biomech Biomed Engin* 27(9):1129–1140): 309 drawings from people at risk of major depression vs. 290 without; eight extracted features, four machine-learning models, best classifier 97.2% accuracy under cross-validation. Same Wuhan group as FBANet. [PM-abs: PMID 37417817, DOI 10.1080/10255842.2023.2231113] — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:gcmbxx:v:27:y:2024:i:9:p:1129-1140)
- 2025 CNN study: 89% accuracy, AUC 0.96 (normal vs. depressed) and AUC 1.00 (moderate vs. severe). Sample size and validation scheme not retrieved. [V-abs; not found in PubMed under this title, so authors, journal, PMID and DOI are not confirmed and the figures rest on the EM-consulte record alone] — [EM-consulte record](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1771628/feature-analysis-of-depression-patients-house-tree)

### Inferences
- Accuracy near 100% for a psychiatric label from a pencil drawing is implausible given that the questionnaires used as ground truth do not agree with each other that well; the likeliest explanations are site/scanner/paper/age confounds (patients drawn in a hospital, controls elsewhere) and leakage. The pattern "small sample → near-perfect; large sample → null" is the classic signature of overfitting.
- The app should not ask users to draw a house, tree or person and should not interpret content symbolically (e.g., "no windows = withdrawal").

### Gaps
- Limitations sections of the high-accuracy HTP papers were not opened; the confound explanation above is an inference, not a documented finding.

## 5. Do people encode emotion in abstract line and colour, and can it be decoded?

### Takeaway
**Promising-but-preliminary for decoding an *intended* emotion from an abstract drawing; well-supported for shared colour–emotion *associations*.** This is the most defensible basis for the product: when people are *asked to draw a named feeling*, their colour and line choices are systematic enough for humans and algorithms to guess the intended emotion well above chance — but far from reliably (roughly 23–51% correct for the algorithm and 32–43% for human observers, against 16.7% chance among six emotions).

### Cited Findings
- Damiano, Gayen, Rezanejad, Banerjee, Banik, Patnaik, Wagemans & Walther (**2023**, not 2021), *Journal of Vision* 23(4): artists and non-artists made abstract colour drawings and line drawings for six emotions (anger, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, wonder); emotion was predicted computationally by comparing each drawing with averages of all other participants' drawings per emotion. "Prediction accuracy was higher for color drawings than line drawings and higher for color drawings by non-artists than by artists." "Anger is generally redder and more densely drawn than other emotions, sadness is more blue and contains more vertical lines." [PM-full: PMID 37010831, *J Vis* 23(4):1; PMC full text opened] — [PMC10080919](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10080919/); DOI 10.1167/jov.23.4.1 (confirmed in PubMed)
- Numbers for that study, now checked against the PMC full text [PM-full]: chance = 1/6 (16.7%). Computational prediction: colour drawings 50.81% for non-artists vs. 39.17% for artists (p < 0.05); line drawings 24.58% for artists and 23.17% for non-artists (no difference); a second line-only analysis using contour features gave 25% and 23.17%. Human observers (244 first-year psychology students at KU Leuven recruited, 242 analysed): colour drawings 43.06% for non-artists' vs. 40% for artists' (p < 0.001); line drawings 31.5% vs. 31.93% (no difference). The "about 41.5% overall" figure in one summary is not in the paper and is not confirmed. The sample-size conflict is resolved: 46 artists (OCAD University) and 45 non-artists (University of Toronto) provided 1,092 drawings; six artists and four non-artists were excluded, so analyses used 40 artists and 41 non-artists (972 drawings) — both earlier reports were right about different stages. — [University of Toronto news](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/researchers-explore-how-we-depict-and-perceive-emotions-through-colour-and-line-visual-art); [Futurum summary](https://www.futurumcareers.com/are-there-rules-for-conveying-emotion-through-art)
- Jonauskaite et al. (2020), *Psychological Science* 31(10), 1245–1260: 4,598 participants, 30 nations, 22 native languages, 20 emotion concepts × 12 colour terms; colour–emotion association patterns were highly similar across nations (average similarity coefficient r = .88), with additional local variation by linguistic and geographic proximity. [PM-abs: PMID 32900287; every figure confirmed in the PubMed abstract] — [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/280269) (DOI 10.1177/0956797620948810)
- Classic line-affect studies: Lundholm (1921), "The affective tone of lines", *Psychological Review* 28, 43–60; Poffenberger & Barrows (1924), "The feeling value of lines", *Journal of Applied Psychology* 8, 187–205 — participants consistently matched line shapes (angular vs. curved, rising vs. falling) to feeling words. NOT retrieved this session; citations, DOIs (10.1037/h0072647; 10.1037/h0073513) and the summary are [MEM; not in PubMed, still unverified].

### Inferences
- **What this evidence licenses:** "People who are asked to draw anger tend to use more red and denser marks; people asked to draw sadness tend to use more blue and vertical lines." It is about *deliberate depiction of a named emotion*.
- **What it does not license:** inferring that a person who happened to use red *is* angry. Jonauskaite's data are word-to-word associations about concepts, not measurements of felt mood; Damiano's participants were told which emotion to depict.
- Design consequence: the task must be an explicit "draw how you feel right now" prompt (expression), and the app must offer a colour palette — colour carried roughly twice the signal of line alone (50.81% vs. 23.17% computational accuracy for non-artists). A black-ink-only canvas throws away the best-supported channel.
- Even the best case (about 51% of six) means the guess is wrong about half the time, so the output must be presented as a guess for the user to confirm or correct.
- Stress, anxiety and depression were not among the six categories studied; mapping to those is an extrapolation.

### Gaps
- No study found testing whether spontaneous drawings made under a *felt* (induced or naturally occurring) emotion are decodable, as opposed to instructed depictions.
- Whether effects hold for 5–30 s finger drawings on a phone is untested.

## 6. Related passive sensing and the plausibility of a motor pathway

### Takeaway
**Promising-but-preliminary.** There is a credible physiological route (psychomotor slowing in depression; arousal effects on timing and force), and typing-dynamics studies show mood-related signal — but the supporting studies are tiny (e.g., N = 9) or rely on longitudinal *within-person* baselines over weeks, which a single 30-second drawing cannot provide.

### Cited Findings
- Zulueta et al. (2018), *JMIR* 20(7):e241 (BiAffect): 19 people with bipolar-spectrum disorder enrolled in an 8-week within-subject study; analysis restricted to the 9 (5 type I, 4 type II) with at least 4 weeks of data; keystroke timing and accelerometer metadata (no text content) predicted weekly clinician ratings — depression (HDRS-17) mixed model conditional R² = .63, P = .01; mania (YMRS) OLS R² = .34, P = .001. [PM-full: PMID 30030209, PMC6076371; R² values confirmed in the abstract, N = 9 of 19 confirmed in the PMC full text] — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2018/7/e241) (DOI 10.2196/jmir.9775)
- Epp, Lippold & Mandryk (2011), CHI '11, pp. 715–724: field study pairing keystroke features (dwell and flight times) with experience-sampled self-reports; classifiers for 15 emotional states; secondary summaries cite accuracies of roughly 77–88% for two-level classification of several states (e.g., about 84% for anger and excitement). Participant count and exact accuracies NOT verified against the paper. [V-sec; not in PubMed — an ACM conference paper, still not confirmed] — [bibliographic record](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-2011-EppLM.html) (DOI 10.1145/1978942.1979046)
- Psychomotor slowing in depression is measurable in pen movements: slower handwriting and less regular drawing velocity in 37 patients vs. 37 controls. [PM-abs: PMID 15049774] — Mergl et al. 2004, see section 2 [citing record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi.dedup.....4fa04655d94540361c15837486fba8b2)
- Stress and graphic motor control: Meulenbroek et al. (2005) tested 10 adults on a graphic task with a distracting sound as stressor, hypothesising increased muscle co-contraction and longer movement times; a related study reported longer reaction/movement times and higher axial pen pressure under cognitive stress [this second clause is not in the PubMed abstract and is not confirmed]. [PM-abs for the first study: Meulenbroek, Van Galen, Hulstijn, Hulstijn & Bloemsaat (2005), *Biol Psychol* 68(3):331–352, PMID 15620798, DOI 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2004.06.002; "Ten right-handed adults", distracting sound in half the trials, and co-variation of movement time with co-contraction confirmed] — [Meulenbroek et al. PDF](https://www.socsci.ru.nl/meulenbroek/Publications/Meulenbroek%20et%20al%202005.pdf)
- Pressure findings are inconsistent: Longstaff & Heath (2000) found people without tremor increased axial pen pressure under mild physical stress and both groups wrote slightly faster, whereas the 56-person mental-workload study found pressure insensitive to load. [Longstaff & Heath 2000: V-sec, not found in PubMed; workload study: PM-abs, PMID 21994184] — [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-influence-of-tremor-on-handwriting/991012821705302368); [DOI 10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pathway is plausible and timing features are the most consistent carriers; pressure is inconsistent across studies and mostly unavailable on phones anyway.
- These signals are far more interpretable as *change from the same person's own baseline* than as an absolute reading. A defensible roadmap item is per-user baselining over repeated check-ins, not single-shot inference.
- Cognitive load, fatigue, caffeine, device, posture, and whether the person is walking all move the same kinematic features; they are not specific to emotion.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found linking acute psychological stress (validated induction) to touchscreen drawing kinematics.
- Epp et al.'s sample size and exact accuracies unverified (not in PubMed).

## 7. Practical recommendations: framing, self-report anchoring, signal capture, regulation

### Takeaway
The defensible product is an **expressive check-in**: the drawing is a reflection prompt and engagement device; a one-tap validated self-report is the actual input that drives the activity suggestion; the AI reading is shown as a tentative guess the user confirms or corrects. Keep it a low-risk general-wellness product with no diagnostic language, disclose the emotion inference, and keep it out of workplace and education deployments in the EU.

### Cited Findings
**Self-report anchors (originally all [MEM]; two of the four are now confirmed in PubMed, the other two are not indexed there):**
- PHQ-4: 4 items, 2 for depression (PHQ-2) and 2 for anxiety (GAD-2), validated ultra-brief screener; asks about the past 2 weeks. Kroenke, Spitzer, Williams & Löwe (2009), *Psychosomatics* 50(6), 613–621 — DOI 10.1176/appi.psy.50.6.613 [PM-abs: PMID 19996233; validated in 2,149 primary-care patients from 15 US clinics; two factors explained 84% of variance; the two-week recall window is not stated in the abstract]
- Affect Grid: single-item 9 × 9 grid capturing valence and arousal in one tap. Russell, Weiss & Mendelsohn (1989), *JPSP* 57(3), 493–502 — DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.57.3.493 [not in PubMed; citation, DOI and the 9 × 9 format remain MEM]
- Self-Assessment Manikin: pictorial, language-free ratings of valence, arousal, dominance. Bradley & Lang (1994), *J. Behav. Ther. Exp. Psychiatry* 25(1), 49–59 — DOI 10.1016/0005-7916(94)90063-9 [PM-abs: PMID 7962581; "non-verbal pictorial assessment technique" measuring pleasure, arousal and dominance confirmed]
- I-PANAS-SF: 10-item cross-culturally validated short PANAS. Thompson (2007), *J. Cross-Cultural Psychology* 38(2), 227–242 — DOI 10.1177/0022022106297301 [not in PubMed; citation and DOI remain MEM]

**Signal capture:**
- W3C Pointer Events: for hardware that does not support pressure, `pressure` "MUST be 0.5 when in the active buttons state and 0 otherwise" — so on most finger-touch phones pressure is a constant, not a measurement. [V-abs] — [MDN: PointerEvent.pressure](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/PointerEvent/pressure)
- Browsers deviate: WebKit on Apple platforms has reported 1.0 (and, per a bug report, 0) for non-pressure-sensitive devices; Firefox on Android has a bug reporting wrong touch pressure. [V-abs] — [WebKit bug 206216](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=206216); [Mozilla bug 1487509](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1487509)
- Timing features (durations, pauses, velocity) are the family with the most consistent support across sections 2 and 6. — [arXiv:2202.12245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245); [DOI 10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8.pdf)

**FDA (US):**
- FDA issued an updated final guidance "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices" on 6 January 2026, superseding the 2019 version; it keeps the two-part test (general-wellness-only intended use + low risk) while broadening examples; a February 2026 CDRH town hall stressed the core principles are unchanged. [V-sec — law-firm summaries; FDA document not opened] — [FDA guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/fda-adapts-with-the-times-on-digital-health-updated-guidances-on-general-wellness-products); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents)

**EU AI Act (status as found November 2026; verify against the Official Journal):**
- Article 5(1)(f) prohibits AI systems "to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions", except for medical or safety reasons. The Commission's guidelines on prohibited practices (published 4 February 2025, non-binding) treat recruitment as part of "workplace", and state the prohibition does not cover emotion recognition in other contexts such as commercial/consumer use. [V-sec] — [Future of Privacy Forum analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2025/02/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-under-eu-ai-act/)

- "Emotion recognition system" is defined (Art. 3(39), Recital 18) as an AI system identifying or inferring emotions or intentions of natural persons **on the basis of their biometric data**; it excludes physical states such as pain or fatigue. Biometric data covers physical, physiological or **behavioural** characteristics. [V-abs] — [Commission AI Act Service Desk, Recital 18](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-18); [Mishcon Article 3](https://mishcon.com/eu-uk-ai-navigator/article-3)

Text as shown on artificialintelligenceact.eu (opened in the verification pass): "Deployers of an emotion recognition system or a biometric categorisation system shall inform the natural persons exposed thereto of the operation of the system, and shall process the personal data in accordance with Regulations (EU) …" (remainder of the paragraph not captured) — [Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/). The application dates in the next sentence were not re-checked in that pass. Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 August 2026 and were **not** delayed by the Digital Omnibus (only the Art. 50(2) machine-readable-marking duty for systems already on the market got a short extension — sources conflict between 2 December 2026 and 2 February 2027). [V-sec] — [Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/cze/legal-updates/eu-ai-act-developments-key-political-agreement-on-the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-implementation-timeline-and-transparency-consultation)
- Emotion recognition systems are an Annex III high-risk category; the Digital Omnibus reportedly deferred Annex III standalone high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027. Sources disagree on whether the Omnibus was formally adopted (one reports Parliament endorsement 16 June 2026 and Council approval 29 June 2026). [V-sec, mixed-quality sources] — [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmoveo6ke00icuxskpgueb0od/ai-omnibus-update); [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)

### Inferences
- **Does the app fall under the AI Act's emotion-recognition definition?** Captured stroke dynamics are behavioural data from an identifiable person and plausibly "biometric data"; a static picture of a drawing is less clearly so. No source found addresses drawings or handwriting specifically. The safe posture is to assume it does: disclose clearly (Art. 50(3)), and never market to employers or schools (Art. 5(1)(f)). A consumer self-care app is outside the prohibition but may still be a high-risk emotion-recognition system once Annex III obligations apply. This is an inference, not legal advice.
- A design in which the *user's own self-report* is the operative input, and the AI only describes the drawing and offers a guess the user can overrule, both weakens the "AI infers your emotion" characterisation and is the scientifically honest design.
- Mood/emotion inferences are sensitive personal data in practice; sending drawings plus stroke data to third-party LLM APIs needs explicit consent and minimisation (GDPR point — not researched here).
- Multi-LLM agreement measures **reliability, not validity**: two models can agree because they share the same folk-psychology priors. Use disagreement as a reason to suppress output, never agreement as proof.
- PHQ-4's two-week recall window makes it a periodic screener, not a per-session mood measure; the Affect Grid or a single mood item fits the per-session one-tap role.

### Gaps
- Art. 5(1)(f) and the first part of Art. 50(3) were read on artificialintelligenceact.eu in the verification pass (an unofficial consolidated site, not the Official Journal; its Art. 5(1)(f) reads "put in place or into the market"). Annex III point 1(c), Art. 3(39) as a standalone page, and all application dates were not opened; those remain from secondary legal sources.
- Final adoption status and exact dates of the Digital Omnibus are disputed between sources.
- Whether the Commission guidelines explicitly name handwriting or keystrokes as behavioural biometrics for emotion recognition: the researcher recalls keystrokes being listed, but this is unverified.
- Current Safari behaviour for Apple Pencil pressure via Pointer Events was not confirmed; test on a device.
- UK, US-state (e.g., biometric privacy statutes) and GDPR analysis not covered.

## Recommendations for the app

- **Reframe the claim.** Say "an expressive check-in that helps you notice how you feel", never "we detect your stress from your handwriting". No peer-reviewed evidence supports single-shot state detection from a short phone drawing, and graphology-style inference is discredited. — [Neter & Ben-Shakhar 1989](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-predictive-validity-of-graphological-inferences-a-meta-analyt/); [Dazzi & Pedrabissi 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/PR0.105.F.1255-1268)
- **Make a one-tap self-report the operative input for recommendations.** Use an Affect Grid or single mood/stress item every session, with PHQ-4 as an optional periodic check; let the drawing enrich, not replace, it. — Russell et al. 1989 (10.1037/0022-3514.57.3.493, DOI from memory; not in PubMed); Kroenke et al. 2009 (10.1176/appi.psy.50.6.613, DOI confirmed in PubMed)
- **Change the task to "draw how you feel right now" with a colour palette.** The only decoding evidence is for deliberate abstract depiction, and colour roughly doubled accuracy over line alone. Do not use handwriting copy tasks or house/tree/person prompts. — [Damiano et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10080919/); [Acta Psychologica 2022 HTP null result](https://doaj.org/article/a1d067ec7ddd43ab98d127d84e4ee9e7)
- **Present the AI output as a tentative guess the user confirms or corrects**, e.g. "This looks intense and dense to me — does 'tense' fit, or is it something else?" Best-case decoding (50.81% for the algorithm, 43% for human observers) is wrong about half the time or more. Log the correction; it is your ground truth. — [University of Toronto summary of Damiano et al.](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/researchers-explore-how-we-depict-and-perceive-emotions-through-colour-and-line-visual-art)
- **Drop decimal emotion scores.** Replace `stress: 0.72` with coarse, hedged labels plus the visible features that prompted them (colour, density, line quality). Numeric scores imply a measurement validity that does not exist for LLMs on this task. — [arXiv:2512.21360 (preprint; similarity to experts, not validity)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21360v1); [Barrett et al. 2019](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/emotional-expressions-reconsidered-challenges-to-inferring-emotion-from-human-facial-movements.html)
- **Constrain the LLM prompt.** Forbid graphological and projective-sign reasoning explicitly; ask the model to describe observable features first and to relate them only to documented colour/line conventions. — [Lilienfeld, Wood & Garb 2000](https://www3.nd.edu/~ghaeffel/Lilienfeld(2000).pdf); [Jonauskaite et al. 2020](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/280269)
- **Capture stroke timing, not just a picture** — per-stroke duration, inter-stroke pauses, speed and speed variability, total ink, canvas coverage. Timing is the feature family with the most consistent support. Label it experimental and do not feed it into user-facing scores yet. — [EMOTHAW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12245); [workload study, 10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-011-0159-8.pdf)
- **Do not rely on pressure.** Record `pressure` and `pointerType`, but treat pressure as valid only for `pointerType === "pen"`; finger touch reports a constant and browsers are inconsistent. — [MDN PointerEvent.pressure](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/PointerEvent/pressure); [WebKit bug 206216](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=206216)
- **Use each user as their own baseline over time.** The more credible passive-sensing results are within-person and longitudinal; show trends only after several check-ins. — [Zulueta et al. 2018](https://www.jmir.org/2018/7/e241)
- **Use multi-LLM agreement only as a suppression filter.** If models disagree, show no guess and just ask the user; do not present agreement as accuracy, since shared priors produce agreement without validity. — [King & Koehler 2000 on illusory correlation, via Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphology)
- **Prefer the 30 s default and standardise it.** The supporting literature uses fixed, comparable tasks; a 5 s sample yields few strokes and no stable timing statistics. This is an inference from the task designs, not a tested threshold. — [EMOTHAW task design](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/emothaw-a-novel-database-for-emotional-state-recognition-from-han/)
- **Stay inside general wellness.** No diagnostic terms ("depression", "anxiety disorder"), no treatment claims, a clear "not a medical device / not a diagnosis" statement, and a route to crisis resources when self-report is severe. — [FDA General Wellness guidance, updated 6 Jan 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download); [Ropes & Gray summary](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/fda-adapts-with-the-times-on-digital-health-updated-guidances-on-general-wellness-products)
- **EU: disclose the emotion inference up front and never sell into workplaces or schools.** Art. 50(3) notice duties have applied since 2 August 2026; Art. 5(1)(f) prohibits emotion inference in workplace and education settings. Plan for possible high-risk (Annex III) classification. — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)
- **Tell judges the validation plan.** Collect drawing + stroke data + same-session self-report (with consent), then test whether any feature predicts the self-report within person, on held-out users. This turns an unvalidated claim into a falsifiable study and avoids the small-sample inflation seen in the literature. — [Nolazco-Flores et al. 2022 as the cautionary example](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8875261/)
- **One-sentence pitch that survives scrutiny:** "Drawing is how you express it, a one-tap check is how we measure it, and the AI is a mirror, not a diagnostician." — grounded in [Damiano et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10080919/) and [Lilienfeld et al. 2000](https://www3.nd.edu/~ghaeffel/Lilienfeld(2000).pdf)
