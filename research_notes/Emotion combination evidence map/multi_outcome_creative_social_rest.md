# Same-sample multi-dimension evidence: creative, music, social, play and rest activities

Scope: studies in which one activity was measured on several mood dimensions **in the same participants**, so the app can see which activities improved a *combination* of its 12 classes (stressed, anxious, angry, restless, sad, lonely, tired, bored, calm, content, energetic, neutral) at once. Builds on `reports/Emotion to activity evidence map.md`, whose combination section says 31 of 34 pair rules are compositions of single-emotion evidence and "no direct trial for any negative pair as a pair" was found.

Verification tags: **[PM-full]** = number read in PubMed Central full text; **[PM-abs]** = number or direction read in the PubMed abstract; **[web]** = from a web search summary of the paper or an index page (abstract-level, not checked against the paper); **[memory]** = from prior knowledge, not re-verified (used sparingly and flagged). PubMed queries, abstracts and PMC full texts were retrieved via NCBI E-utilities; scripts and raw outputs are in the scratchpad (`combo_creative/`).

Class mapping used: depression/dejection → sad; tension/anxiety/STAI/GAD → anxious; anger-hostility → angry; fatigue/sleepiness → tired; vigor/energy → energetic; positive affect/happiness → content; perceived stress/cortisol → stressed; UCLA/De Jong → lonely; "interest" (inverse) → bored. Negative affect (NA) is **not** a single class: it goes in `other_dimensions` unless a study reports subscales.

## 1. Which activities improve several dimensions together in the same sample?

### Takeaway
Same-sample multi-outcome data exist for most of the target activities. They show a consistent pattern: **positive affect (content) and global negative affect move together after almost any engaging activity, but specific negative states (anxiety, depression, loneliness, worry) often do not move with them.** The best-supported tested pairs are: **lonely+sad+anxious** (empathic phone calls, Kahlon 2021); **anxious+sad** (group drumming over 10 weeks; laughter/humour meta-analysis; music therapy for depression); **stressed+tired** (PMR plus music in ICU nurses, a mixed-state sample); **tired+energetic** (a 10-minute nap); **angry+energetic** (comedy video, n = 10); and **stressed+content+energetic** (therapy dog drop-in). Loneliness often did **not** improve alongside mood. It was unchanged after a single online dance class and after choir in non-lonely students. Best-possible-self writing reliably raises positive affect but not negative affect or depression.

### Cited Findings

**Art-making, drawing, colouring, clay**
- In 34 cancer caregivers, a single 45-min session of colouring *or* open-studio art therapy raised positive affect, creative agency and self-efficacy, and lowered negative affect, anxiety, perceived stress and burnout, in both conditions [PM-abs]. A larger effect came with more art-making experience [PM-abs]. — [Kaimal et al. 2019, PMID 31557665](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejon.2019.08.006)
- In 39 healthy adults, 45 min of art-making lowered salivary cortisol, and participants described it as relaxing and flow-like. Cortisol change did not differ by prior art experience, medium or gender [PM-abs]. Only one quantitative dimension was measured. — [Kaimal, Ray & Muniz 2016, PMID 27695158](https://doi.org/10.1080/07421656.2016.1166832)
- In 50 adults (18–30), making art reduced negative mood and anxiety more than viewing and sorting art prints, on all three measures (all p < .005) [web]. — [Bell & Robbins 2007, Art Therapy 24(2):71–75](https://nufind.nu.edu.sa/EdsRecord/eric,EJ777027)
- In 102 adults after a negative mood induction, clay handling improved mood more than ball handling [web]. No effect size was available. — [Kimport & Robbins 2012](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07421656.2012.680048)
- In children who recalled a disappointing event, drawing for **distraction** improved mood more than drawing to **vent** or copying a drawing [PM-abs]. — [Drake & Winner 2013, PMID 22963448](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2012.720567). The adult version reports the same direction (distraction > venting after a sadness induction) [web]. — [Drake & Winner 2012, PACA 6:251–261, doi:10.1037/a0026909](https://typeset.io/pdf/confronting-sadness-through-art-making-distraction-is-more-4yr4tzvok3.pdf)
- Colouring once a day for a week lowered depression, anxiety and stress scores, with no change in a logic-puzzle control [web]. This comes from a secondary review summary of Flett et al. 2017, and the original abstract was not retrieved. — [Holt review, UWE repository](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/9294488)
- Meta-analysis of 50 RCTs of active visual art therapy (2,766 participants): SMD in change from baseline 0.38 (95% CI 0.26–0.51), posttest SMD 0.19 (0.12–0.26). Only 18% of 217 outcomes improved vs 1% in controls, and 81% showed no improvement. Most outcomes were depression, anxiety, self-esteem and quality of life, and study quality was low [PM-abs]. — [Joschko et al. 2024, PMID 39264631](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.28709)

**Writing (best possible self, gratitude)**
- BPS meta-analysis (29 studies, N = 2,909): positive affect d+ = 0.511 (95% CI 0.257–0.765), wellbeing 0.325, optimism 0.334. **Negative affect d+ = 0.192 (CI −0.328 to 0.712, ns)** and **depression d+ = 0.115 (CI −0.272 to 0.502, ns, k = 3)** [PM-full]. Against active controls, the negative-affect effect was "practically null" (d+ = −0.047) [PM-full]. — [Carrillo et al. 2019, PMID 31545815](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222386)
- Preregistered BPS RCT (n = 240 students): large direct effects on positive expectancies and positive mood, only a small *indirect* effect on negative mood, and an indirect effect on anxiety symptoms at 1 week via expectancies [PM-abs]. — [Booth et al. 2025, PMID 40402638](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001481)
- Gratitude meta-analysis: psychological well-being d = .31 vs measurement-only control, but **anxiety d = .11 (CI −.08 to .31, ns, k = 5)** vs alternative activity [PM-abs]. — [Davis et al. 2016, PMID 26575348](https://doi.org/10.1037/cou0000107)
- Gratitude listing: "the effect on positive affect appeared to be the most robust finding". Other outcomes improved "across several, though not all" measures [PM-abs]. — [Emmons & McCullough 2003, PMID 12585811](https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-3514.84.2.377)

**Music listening, music therapy, playing an instrument**
- 104 RCTs (9,617 participants): music interventions reduced psychological stress outcomes (d = .545) and physiological ones (d = .380), with heart rate d = .456 [PM-abs]. Psychological stress outcomes include restlessness, anxiety and nervousness (as framed by the authors). — [de Witte et al. 2020, PMID 31167611](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2019.1627897)
- Music *therapy*, 47 studies: stress-related outcomes d = .723 (95% CI .51–.94), with larger effects in non-RCTs and against waitlist controls [PM-abs]. — [de Witte et al. 2022, PMID 33176590](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2020.1846580)
- Cochrane review of music therapy for depression (9 studies): patient-reported depression SMD −0.85 (−1.37 to −0.34), and music therapy plus TAU was superior for **anxiety** and functioning. **Quality of life ns** (SMD 0.32, CI −0.17 to 0.80) [PM-abs]. — [Aalbers et al. 2017, PMID 29144545](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004517.pub3)
- Everyday music listening (EMA, n = 55 students): lower subjective stress (p = .010). The largest stress drop and lower cortisol came when "relaxation" was the stated reason (p ≤ .001). Energizing music raised alpha-amylase and relaxing music lowered it [PM-abs]. — [Linnemann et al. 2015, PMID 26142566](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2015.06.008)
- Daily 30-min music listening for 3 weeks in healthcare workers (n = 45): stress showed only a trend (p = .07) and well-being was ns [PM-abs]. — [Raglio et al. 2020, PMID 30942385](https://doi.org/10.1093/pubmed/fdz030)
- **Stressed AND tired sample:** in ICU nurses (RCT, n = 56), 20-min sessions of progressive muscle relaxation with music over 8 weeks lowered **stress and fatigue** at weeks 8 and 12 vs attention control (p < .05) [PM-abs]. — [Ozgundondu & Gok Metin 2019, PMID 31371164](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iccn.2019.07.007)
- Group recreational music-making (drumming and keyboard, 6 sessions) in 112 long-term-care workers: significant reductions in "multiple" POMS mood dimensions, total mood disturbance and burnout dimensions [PM-abs]. The abstract does not say which POMS subscales changed. — [Bittman et al. 2003, PMID 14686266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14686266/). Replicated in 75 nursing students [PM-abs]. — [Bittman et al. 2004, PMID 16646877](https://doi.org/10.2202/1548-923x.1044)
- Remote personalised music listening in 10 lonely older adults: median loneliness and well-being measures all moved in an improved direction (pilot, no inferential statistics in the abstract) [PM-abs]. — [Haddad et al. 2024, PMID 38993691](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.osep.2024.03.001)

**Singing, choir, drumming**
- One hour of choir singing in 193 people affected by cancer (single arm): significant time effects on all twelve mood items, with positive affect up and negative affect down (all p < .001), and lower stress. Cortisol, beta-endorphin and oxytocin fell, and several cytokines rose (p < .01) [PM-full]. — [Fancourt et al. 2016, PMID 27170831](https://doi.org/10.3332/ecancer.2016.631)
- Choir members (amateur choir; n not in abstract): singing raised positive affect and S-IgA and lowered negative affect. **Listening to choral music raised negative affect** (while lowering cortisol) [PM-abs]. — [Kreutz et al. 2004, PMID 15669447](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-004-0006-9)
- 10 weeks of group drumming in mental-health service users (n = 30 drumming vs 15 control): depression −3.41 (SE 0.62), anxiety −2.21 (SE 0.50; interaction F = 3.63, p < .05), wellbeing +6.14 and social resilience +10.59, maintained at 3 months. **Perceived stress fell in both groups**, with no condition × time interaction (p = .131) [PM-full]. — [Fancourt et al. 2016 PLoS One, PMID 26974430](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0151136)
- Community of Voices cluster RCT (390 older adults, 6 months of choir): loneliness improved (p = .02, ES 0.34) and interest in life improved (p = .008, ES 0.39). There was no effect on cognitive or physical outcomes [PM-abs]. — [Johnson et al. 2020, PMID 30412233](https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gby132)
- Choir for international students (n = 53, non-randomised): **loneliness ns** (interaction p = .351), which the authors note "was not elevated at baseline". Belonging improved (interaction p = .021) [PM-full]. — [Han et al. 2025, PMID 40426352](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs15050575)
- 2-year choir RCT vs an active health-education control (n = 200 older adults): depression and anxiety screens fell **equally** in both arms [PM-abs]. — [Ng et al. 2025, PMID 40449223](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.05.061)
- Sing4Health RCT (n = 149 older adults, 34 sessions): less decline in perceived physical health. Mental-health changes were mediated via positive affect, and participants with very low cognition reported *more* anxiety and depression afterwards [PM-abs]. — [Galinha et al. 2023, PMID 34355628](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2021.1955117)

**Dance**
- Dance/DMT meta-analysis (41 studies, N = 2,374): overall d = 0.60, and "DMT decreases depression and anxiety and increases quality of life" [PM-abs]. The full text cites the prior Koch 2014 meta as anxiety SMD 0.44 and depression SMD 0.36 [PM-full, secondary citation]. — [Koch et al. 2019, PMID 31481910](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01806)
- One 60-min online dance class (n = 47, single arm): positive affect and self-esteem rose, and negative affect and BDI depressive symptoms fell. **UCLA loneliness ns** (p > .05), though social and community connectedness rose. Larger NA decreases went with larger connectedness gains (r = −0.317, p = .030) [PM-full]. — [Humphries et al. 2023, PMID 36647172](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-022-01034-w)

**Cooking, crafts, leisure**
- Systematic review of 11 cooking-intervention studies: positive influences on socialisation, self-esteem, QoL and affect, but evidence quality was weak (non-randomised, unvalidated tools) [PM-abs]. — [Farmer et al. 2018, PMID 29121776](https://doi.org/10.1177/1090198117736352)
- Crochet survey (n = 8,391, retrospective self-report): 89.5% felt calmer and 82% happier. Mood rose from M = 4.19 before to M = 5.78 after (z = −69.86, p < .001, r = −0.56) [PM-abs]. — [Burns & Van Der Meer 2021, PMID 32245337](https://doi.org/10.1177/1757913920911961)
- EMA of everyday leisure (n = 115, 6 prompts/day for 3 days): during leisure, people had more positive and less negative mood, **more interest (less boredom), less stress** and lower heart rate [PM-abs]. — [Zawadzki et al. 2015, PMID 25724635](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-015-9694-3)

**Casual video games**
- Bejeweled II RCT: EEG and self-report consistent with better mood, and HRV consistent with less stress [PM-abs]. No numbers in the abstract. — [Russoniello et al. 2009, PMID 19592761](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19592761/)
- Casual games 3×/week, 30 min, for 1 month vs an NIMH web-page control (n = 30 vs 29) reduced PHQ-9 depression [web]. A related ECU study reported reduced STAI anxiety in depressed participants with comorbid anxiety [web]. Press figures (57% and 20% reductions) were not checked against the papers. — [Russoniello, Fish & O'Brien 2013, Games Health J 2(6), doi:10.1089/g4h.2013.0010](https://scholars.ecu.edu/display/N750157742)
- Adaptive Tetris (flow) while waiting for uncertain news (Study 3, n = 309): positive emotion rose and negative emotion fell, but **worry was not reduced** [PM-abs]. — [Rankin, Walsh & Sweeny 2019, PMID 30265082](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000479)
- Review of 13 casual-game studies (6 on anxiety, 2 on depression, 4 on stress/low mood): "promising effects" [PM-abs]. — [Pine et al. 2020, PMID 32053021](https://doi.org/10.1089/g4h.2019.0132). Review of 28 studies of commercial games: games reduced stress and anxiety, "even single and short sessions" [PM-abs]. — [Pallavicini et al. 2021, PMID 34398795](https://doi.org/10.2196/28150). During COVID, gaming mitigated stress, anxiety, depression and loneliness in adolescents and young adults, but was harmful for at-risk (especially male) youth [PM-abs]. — [Pallavicini et al. 2022, PMID 35639118](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2021.0252)

**Humour, comedy, laughter**
- Comedy video (n = 10 women): POMS **anger-hostility fell and vigor rose** significantly. A tragedy video raised depression-dejection [PM-abs]. — [Sakuragi et al. 2002, PMID 12148458](https://doi.org/10.2114/jpa.21.159)
- 20 women, within-subjects, 20 min each of a humorous video, music, cycling or quiet sitting: state anxiety fell in all four conditions, with the largest effect size for humour [web]. — [Szabo, Ainsworth & Danks 2005, Humor 18(3), doi:10.1515/humr.2005.18.3.235](https://doi.org/10.1515/humr.2005.18.3.235)
- Laughter/humour RCT meta-analysis (10 studies, n = 814): significant decreases in **depression and anxiety** and better sleep quality, with depression benefiting more from long-term interventions [PM-abs]. — [Zhao et al. 2019, PMID 30882915](https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.14000)
- Laughter-therapy meta-analysis: these therapies improve depression, and "simulated" laughter beat humour-based laughter. Study quality was low [PM-abs]. — [van der Wal & Kok 2019, PMID 31029483](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2019.02.018)
- Laughter yoga in people with depression (RCT, n = 50; DASS-21 measured): **only depression** (and mental-health QoL) improved more than TAU, per the abstract [PM-abs]. — [Bressington et al. 2019, PMID 30711868](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.01.030)
- Depressed older adults (n = 84): laughter yoga and music both lowered depression and anxiety (p < .001). Stress fell immediately but rose again by 1 month [PM-abs]. — [Tabei et al. 2025, PMID 39107930](https://doi.org/10.1080/13607863.2024.2385454)
- Distance laughter therapy vs watching a 50-min entertainment TV show (n = 22 mothers): **both arms** reduced depression and state anxiety (within-group p ≤ .038). Only laughter therapy reduced parenting stress, and between-group differences were ns [PM-full]. — [Ko & Park 2023, PMID 37450484](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0288246)

**Social interaction, phone calls, kindness**
- Empathy-focused phone calls (RCT, n = 240 homebound adults, 4 weeks): UCLA loneliness improved 1.1 (d = 0.48, p < .001), PHQ-8 depression 1.5 (d = 0.31), GAD-7 anxiety 1.8 (d = 0.35) and SF mental health 2.6 (d = 0.46). **De Jong loneliness ns** (d = 0.17, p = .06), and the reduction in the proportion with mild-or-worse depression was ns [PM-full]. — [Kahlon et al. 2021, PMID 33620417](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0113)
- Talking to a stranger on a commute gave a more positive experience than solitude, contrary to people's predictions [PM-abs]. — [Epley & Schroeder 2014, PMID 25019381](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037323). More weak-tie interactions went with more happiness *and* belonging on the same day (observational) [PM-abs]. — [Sandstrom & Dunn 2014, PMID 24769739](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167214529799)
- Kindness meta-analysis (27 studies, N = 4,045): well-being δ = 0.28, with no publication bias detected [web]. — [Curry et al. 2018, JESP 76:320–329](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/30561/)
- Prosocial vs self-focused acts over 6 weeks (n = 473): prosocial acts raised flourishing via **more positive and fewer negative emotions**. Self-kindness did not change PE, NE or flourishing [PM-abs]. — [Nelson et al. 2016, PMID 27100366](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000178)
- 7-day kindness (n = 683): happiness rose equally for kindness to strong ties, weak ties or self, or observing kindness [PM-abs]. — [Rowland & Curry 2019, PMID 29702043](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224545.2018.1469461)
- Adolescents (RCT, n = 97): kindness overall did not change PA, NA or stress vs controls. **Only altruistic adolescents** showed PA↑, NA↓ and stress↓ [PM-abs]. — [Tashjian et al. 2021, PMID 33278014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-020-01362-3)
- Prosociality–well-being meta-analysis: r = .13 (K = 201). The link was stronger for eudaimonic than hedonic well-being, and more modest for "psychological malfunctioning" [PM-abs]. — [Hui et al. 2020, PMID 32881540](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000298)
- Kind acts in 9–11-year-olds: well-being rose in both arms, and peer acceptance rose more with kindness [PM-abs]. — [Layous et al. 2012, PMID 23300546](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0051380)
- Loneliness intervention meta-analysis: in randomised studies, interventions addressing maladaptive social cognition worked best [PM-abs]. — [Masi et al. 2011, PMID 20716644](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868310377394)

**Pets / animal-assisted**
- 20-min dog interaction (n = 132 students, 3 arms): STAI fell by 13.73 with dog and handler, 12.98 with dog only and 2.02 with handler only. Condition effects: anxiety η² = .30, well-being η² = .27, UMACL mood η² = .068 [PM-full]. — [Grajfoner et al. 2017, PMID 28475132](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph14050483)
- Drop-in therapy dog session (n = 246 pre/post): **stress fell and happiness and energy rose**. Against a delayed-treatment control ~10 h later, negative affect, perceived social support and perceived stress improved more, but "effects after several hours are small" [PM-abs]. — [Ward-Griffin et al. 2018, PMID 29528189](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.2804)
- AAT in long-term care (n = 45, 6 weeks): UCLA loneliness fell vs no AAT. Only loneliness was measured [PM-abs]. — [Banks & Banks 2002, PMID 12084804](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/57.7.m428)

**Nostalgia, awe**
- Loneliness increased nostalgia, and nostalgia in turn increased perceived social support (4 studies). The effect was "particularly apparent among resilient persons" [PM-abs]. — [Zhou et al. 2008, PMID 19000213](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02194.x)
- Nostalgia is triggered by negative mood and loneliness, and bolsters social bonds, positive self-regard and positive affect [PM-abs]. — [Wildschut et al. 2006, PMID 17059314](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.91.5.975)
- Induced boredom raised nostalgia, and nostalgia mediated the effect of boredom on meaningfulness (6 studies) [PM-abs]. — [van Tilburg et al. 2013, PMID 23163710](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030442)
- **Contradiction:** in five 14-day diary studies (N = 504, 6,004 reports), daily nostalgia went with *lower* PA and *higher* NA, and more so on lonely days ("nostalgia did not attenuate, but rather exaggerated" loneliness effects) [PM-abs]. — [Newman & Sachs 2020, PMID 32982886](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.02185)
- Nostalgia weakened the link between loneliness and low social confidence and low approach goals, "particularly at high levels of loneliness" [PM-abs]. — [Abeyta et al. 2020, PMID 32655445](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01219)
- Awe walks (15 min weekly for 8 weeks, n = 60 older adults): more joy and prosocial positive emotions, and greater decreases in daily distress. **GAD-type anxiety (p = .17) and depression (p = .64) change ns**, with baseline scores minimal [PM-full]. — [Sturm et al. 2022, PMID 32955293](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000876)
- Awe priming and an awe-inspiring view reduced daily stress, mediated by vastness appraisals, and raised life satisfaction (6 studies) [PM-abs]. — [Bai et al. 2021, PMID 33764120](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000267)

**Naps**
- After 5 h of restricted sleep (n = 24, 5-condition crossover), a 10-min nap immediately improved sleep latency, **subjective sleepiness, fatigue, vigor** and cognitive performance, with some benefits lasting 155 min. A 30-min nap caused sleep inertia first, and a 5-min nap gave few benefits [PM-abs]. — [Brooks & Lack 2006, PMID 16796222](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831)
- A short nap (stage-2 sleep) after mild sleep restriction (n = 10) improved subjective alertness and performance vs the post-lunch dip of the no-nap condition [PM-abs]. — [Hayashi et al. 2005, PMID 16124661](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16124661/)

### Inferences
- "Mood improved" in single-session studies (singing, dance, dog, Tetris, colouring) is mostly a PA↑/NA↓ result. The app should credit these activities to **content** and to the *global* negative load, not automatically to each specific negative class.
- Activities with both a social and an affective channel (phone calls, choir over months, group drumming) are the only ones with same-sample evidence that **lonely** improves together with **sad/anxious**. A single social-ish session (online dance) improved mood but not loneliness. Loneliness likely needs repeated contact.
- Writing interventions (BPS, gratitude) are "add positive" tools: good for low content or low energetic, weak for sad, anxious or stressed combinations.

### Gaps
- No same-sample subscale data were retrieved for reading, knitting (beyond the crochet survey), baking or cooking (beyond the review), or painting alone.
- Bittman's POMS subscale values, Russoniello's POMS data and Kreutz's sample size were not in the abstracts.
- The Bell & Robbins, Kimport & Robbins, Drake & Winner 2012, Flett 2017 and Szabo 2005 numbers are abstract-level web summaries only.

## 2. Meta-analyses that report subscale-level effects

### Takeaway
Meta-analytic subscale data exist for: music (stress, depression plus anxiety), laughter (depression plus anxiety), dance/DMT (depression plus anxiety), BPS (PA vs NA vs depression), gratitude (well-being vs anxiety) and kindness (well-being, with PE/NE as mechanisms). **Art-therapy meta-analysis (Joschko 2024) found that 81% of outcomes did not improve.** No meta-analysis reports loneliness *and* depression for the same loneliness interventions in the sources retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Music therapy plus TAU for depression: depression SMD −0.85, anxiety superior to TAU, QoL ns [PM-abs]. — [Aalbers 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004517.pub3)
- Music interventions on stress: psychological d = .545, physiological d = .380 [PM-abs]. — [de Witte 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2019.1627897)
- Laughter/humour: depression and anxiety both reduced, 10 RCTs [PM-abs]. — [Zhao 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.14000)
- DMT: depression and anxiety reduced. The previous meta reported anxiety SMD 0.44 and depression 0.36 [PM-abs / PM-full]. — [Koch 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01806)
- BPS: PA d = .511 significant, NA d = .192 ns, depression d = .115 ns [PM-full]. — [Carrillo 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222386)
- Gratitude: well-being d = .31, anxiety d = .11 ns [PM-abs]. — [Davis 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/cou0000107)
- Visual art therapy: SMD 0.38 change-from-baseline, but only 18% of outcomes improved [PM-abs]. — [Joschko 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.28709)
- Kindness: well-being δ = .28 [web]. — [Curry 2018](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/30561/). Prosociality: r = .13, weaker for "malfunctioning" outcomes [PM-abs]. — [Hui 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000298)
- Casual games: systematic reviews only, with no pooled subscale effect sizes [PM-abs]. — [Pine 2020](https://doi.org/10.1089/g4h.2019.0132); [Pallavicini 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/28150)

### Inferences
- For the app, the meta-analytic pair evidence is strongest for **sad+anxious** (music therapy, laughter, DMT). These are multi-session programmes, so a single session will deliver less.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis was found that reports pet interaction across stress, mood and loneliness. A 2018 meta on animal-assisted stress (Ein et al.) is known from memory but was not retrieved.
- No pooled data were found for nostalgia or awe on several outcomes.

## 3. Baseline moderation: does the activity help more when the state starts higher?

### Takeaway
Yes, for several activities. Singing benefit scales strongly with baseline stress and low baseline mood. Nostalgia's social benefit is clearest at high loneliness (in experiments), but daily-life nostalgia worsens affect on lonely days. Kindness helped only altruistic adolescents. Choir did nothing for loneliness when loneliness was not elevated. These findings support intensity-weighted scoring, with ceiling and floor caveats.

### Cited Findings
- See the "Baseline moderation findings" table below. Every row is sourced there.

### Inferences
- Pre-post designs make "bigger gain at higher baseline" partly regression to the mean. Only controlled comparisons (Han 2025 null; Abeyta 2020 moderation) separate a true moderation effect from that artefact.

### Gaps
- No moderation analysis was found for art-making by baseline negativity in PubMed-accessible text. Kaimal 2016 found no moderation by prior experience, media or gender.

## 4. Studies in mixed-state samples

### Takeaway
A few samples fit the app's combinations. They are: lonely, depressed and anxious homebound adults (Kahlon, positive on all three); stressed and fatigued ICU nurses (PMR plus music, positive on both); depressed people with anxiety (casual games [web]; laughter yoga, where only depression moved); depressed older adults (laughter yoga or music, depression and anxiety down); and lonely older adults (music therapy pilot; choir, loneliness down). No "bored AND lonely students" intervention study was found. Correlational work links boredom and loneliness to problematic internet use, but tests no activity.

### Cited Findings
- [Kahlon 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0113): loneliness d .48, depression d .31, anxiety d .35 [PM-full].
- [Ozgundondu 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iccn.2019.07.007): stress and fatigue both lower at weeks 8 and 12 [PM-abs].
- [Tabei 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/13607863.2024.2385454): depressed elderly, depression and anxiety p < .001, stress gains not sustained [PM-abs].
- [Bressington 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.01.030): depressed adults, depression improved, anxiety and stress not reported as improved [PM-abs].
- [Johnson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gby132): older adults, loneliness ES .34 and interest in life ES .39 [PM-abs].
- [Haddad 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.osep.2024.03.001): lonely older adults, all measures improved in direction (pilot, n = 10) [PM-abs].

### Gaps
- No bored+restless or angry+sad sample with an activity intervention was found.

## 5. Combination-specific findings (sad music, comedy, games)

### Takeaway
- **Sad music when sad (± lonely):** a 772-person survey found that sad music is liked in a mood-congruent way, more so by people with high empathy and low emotional stability. Its rewards include emotion regulation and consolation, and the most frequent emotion evoked was nostalgia, not sadness. In depressed people, however, music use in groups is linked to "group rumination". Sad music for sad+lonely is defensible only for non-ruminators, ideally steered toward nostalgic and consoling content.
- **Comedy when angry+sad:** a comedy video reduced POMS anger and raised vigor (n = 10). Depression-dejection was not reported as changed after comedy, but rose after a tragedy video. Humour reduced state anxiety most among four 20-min activities. Laughter programmes reduce depression and anxiety over weeks. Same-session evidence covers anger and vigor only.
- **Games for bored+restless:** there are no direct studies. Flow (Tetris) improved PE and NE but not worry, and leisure EMA links leisure to more interest and less stress. The pair rule for bored+restless remains an extrapolation.

### Cited Findings
- [Taruffi & Koelsch 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0110490): N = 772; liking of sad music correlates with empathy (r = .114) and negatively with emotional stability (r = −.123); nostalgia most frequent [PM-full].
- [Garrido et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00490): people with depression more likely to engage in group rumination with music [PM-abs].
- [Kreutz 2004](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-004-0006-9): *listening* to choral music raised NA, while *singing* lowered it [PM-abs]. This argues for active over passive music when sad.
- [Sakuragi 2002](https://doi.org/10.2114/jpa.21.159); [Szabo 2005](https://doi.org/10.1515/humr.2005.18.3.235); [Rankin 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000479); [Zawadzki 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-015-9694-3) — as above.

### Inferences
- Rule suggestion: for sad+lonely, prefer **active/social** music (singing, a call while sharing music) over solitary sad-music loops. If sad music is chosen, frame it as nostalgic and consoling, and avoid it for users flagged as ruminators.

### Gaps
- There is no experiment of comedy in an induced angry+sad state, and none of games in an induced bored+restless state.
