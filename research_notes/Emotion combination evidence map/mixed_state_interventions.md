# Interventions and regulation strategies for mixed or co-occurring emotional states

Scope: direct or near-direct evidence for the combination rules R10–R27 in `research_notes/Emotion to activity evidence map/emotion_models_and_matching_logic.md`, and for general principles of regulating several emotions at once. Class ids are the app's 12 classes (stressed, anxious, angry, restless, sad, lonely, tired, bored, calm, content, energetic, neutral). Where a study used "happy" or "excited" it is mapped to content or energetic.

Verification tags: **[PM-abs]** = number or claim read in the PubMed abstract retrieved through NCBI E-utilities in this pass; **[PM-full]** = read in PMC full text (none used in this pass); **[web]** = taken from a web search summary of the abstract, not confirmed in PubMed; **[memory]** = from prior knowledge, not re-verified here. No PMID or DOI below is invented; every PMID and DOI tagged [PM-abs] was printed by efetch. DOIs not printed by efetch are marked "not verified".

Overall finding: there are almost no trials that recruit people *because* two specific momentary emotions are elevated together. The usable direct evidence takes three forms: (a) interventions tested on **both outcomes** in the same trial (transdiagnostic protocols, exercise, mindfulness, loneliness programmes that also measure depression and anxiety); (b) a few experiments whose induced state is itself a blend (pre-performance anxiety with arousal; anger recall that is also sad; loneliness that increases nostalgia); and (c) daily-life studies of how people regulate when several emotions and strategies co-occur. Most of these confirm the direction of the earlier rules. Several refine them, and two findings contradict specific parts.

## 1. Anxious + sad (mixed anxiety and low mood)

### Takeaway
Approaches that target shared negative affect (transdiagnostic emotion-regulation protocols, physical activity, mindfulness) reduce anxiety and depression by about the same amount in the same trials. Behavioural activation, the main "sad" tool, reliably lowers depression, but in digital form it did not significantly lower anxiety. So R17 should lead with movement and mindfulness, which work on both, and treat PLEASANT/BA as the sadness-specific add-on.

### Cited Findings
- Unified Protocol (UP) meta-analysis: 15 studies, 1,244 participants. Large reductions in anxiety, depression, GAD, OCD, panic, social anxiety and BPD symptoms; moderate increases in adaptive and decreases in maladaptive ER strategies [PM-abs]. The abstract reports no pooled g — [Sakiris & Berle 2019, PMID 31271848](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2019.101751)
- UP meta-analysis: 19 RCTs plus 13 uncontrolled trials, 2,183 patients. Combined depression-plus-anxiety g = 0.452 in RCTs, g = 0.756 pre–post, g = 1.113 at 3–6-month follow-up. Effects "do not differ across the anxiety and depression self-report measures"; UP beat passive and active controls [PM-abs] — [Carlucci et al. 2021, PMID 34098412](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.101999)
- Unguided mindfulness self-help: 83 RCTs. Depression g = −0.23, anxiety g = −0.25, stress g = −0.41, wellbeing g = 0.34. Depression and anxiety effects were *not* retained at follow-up, while stress and wellbeing were. Digital delivery gave smaller effects than non-digital on depression [PM-abs] — [Taylor et al. 2021, PMID 34537665](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102078)
- Physical activity umbrella review: 97 reviews, 1,039 trials, 128,119 participants. Median effect on depression −0.43, anxiety −0.42, psychological distress −0.60. Higher intensity gave larger improvements; longer programmes gave smaller effects [PM-abs]. Note that 77 of 97 reviews were rated "critically low" quality — [Singh et al. 2023, PMID 36796860](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106195)
- A single bout of exercise reduced state anxiety by a small amount: g = 0.16 across 36 RCTs, with floor effects from low-anxious samples [PM-abs] — [Ensari et al. 2015, PMID 25899389](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.22370)
- Digital behavioural activation (17 RCTs, 12 meta-analysed): significant depression reduction at 2, 3 and 6 months and lower stress at 3 months. Anxiety was **not** significantly reduced at 3 months (P = .08) or 6 months (P = .24) [PM-abs] — [Jia et al. 2025, PMID 40526910](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)
- Group BA in adolescents (quasi-experimental, n = 139, 8 × 90 min): anxiety d = 0.42 post and 0.82 at 3 months; depression d = 0.43 at 3 months [PM-abs]. This shows face-to-face BA *can* move anxiety, unlike the digital pooled result — [Zhang et al. 2025, PMID 41444911](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13034-025-01012-1)
- "Undoing effect": after anxiety-induced cardiovascular reactivity, contentment and amusement films produced faster recovery than neutral or sad films (n = 170) [PM-abs]. A positive state can cut anxiety arousal, which supports pairing calming with a small pleasant element — [Fredrickson et al. 2000, PMID 21731120](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1010796329158)
- Survey and therapist ratings: exercise is the most effective mood-regulating behaviour, and combining relaxation, stress management, cognitive and exercise techniques is the best general strategy for a bad mood [PM-abs] — [Thayer et al. 1994, PMID 7983582](https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-3514.67.5.910)

### Inferences
- Shared-mechanism activities (movement, mindfulness or acceptance, and an emotion-regulation skill) are the evidence-backed "both" options. BA/PLEASANT is sadness-weighted.
- The UP and mindfulness evidence comes from multi-week programmes. A 5–30 min session is a micro-dose of them, so the expected momentary effect is smaller.

### Gaps
- No momentary (single-session) trial selected people high on both anxiety and sadness.
- No exercise RCT was found that pools effects specifically for *comorbid* anxiety-depression samples. PubMed returned only case reports for "mixed anxiety depressive disorder".

## 2. Stressed + tired (tense-tiredness, exhaustion)

### Takeaway
Recovery research confirms the "lower tension cheaply" principle, with three refinements. A brief walk lifts **energy** but does not reduce **fatigue** unless it is low-to-moderate intensity and lasts more than 20 minutes. A 10-minute nap is the best short nap after restricted sleep, while a 30-minute nap causes sleep inertia. Exhausted, stressed people find detachment hardest, and pleasurable leisure protects that detachment.

### Cited Findings
- Acute exercise (16 studies, n = 678; mostly 21–40 min of moderate aerobic exercise): energy Δ = 0.47 (95% CI 0.39–0.56), but fatigue Δ = 0.03 (−0.08 to 0.13). Fatigue fell only when energy rose substantially after low-to-moderate intensity exercise lasting more than 20 min [web] — [Loy, O'Connor & Dishman 2013, *Fatigue*](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266)
- In young adults reporting persistent fatigue, vigor rose after both low- and moderate-intensity cycling sessions. Fatigue fell after **low**-intensity sessions only (weeks 3 and 6) [PM-abs] — [Dishman et al. 2010, PMID 20409016](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2010.01014.x)
- Chronic exercise raised energy and lowered fatigue (mean Δ = 0.37). With placebo controls and exercise alone there was no effect [PM-abs] — [Puetz et al. 2006, PMID 17073524](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.866)
- Energy and fatigue behave as independent unipolar states, so changes in one often occur without the other (hypothesis paper with supporting factor-analytic evidence) [PM-abs] — [Loy et al. 2018, PMID 29523293](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mehy.2018.02.014)
- Micro-breaks (≤10 min; 22 samples, N = 2,335): vigor d = 0.36, fatigue d = 0.35, performance d = 0.16 (n.s.). Longer breaks are needed after highly depleting tasks [PM-abs] — [Albulescu et al. 2022, PMID 36044424](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)
- Naps after restricting sleep to about 5 h (n = 24): 5 min gave few benefits. **10 min** gave immediate gains in sleepiness, fatigue, vigor and cognition lasting up to 155 min. 20 min gave gains from 35 min after waking. 30 min first impaired alertness (sleep inertia) [PM-abs] — [Brooks & Lack 2006, PMID 16796222](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831)
- Replication after 4.7 h of sleep (n = 12): a 10-min nap improved alertness at once for the full hour, while a 30-min nap first worsened it [PM-abs] — [Tietzel & Lack 2001, PMID 11322712](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/24.3.293)
- Detachment interventions (30 studies, N = 3,725): d = 0.36. They were more effective at higher dose and in participants with existing health or recovery impairments [PM-abs] — [Karabinski et al. 2021, PMID 34096763](https://doi.org/10.1037/ocp0000280)
- Detachment meta-analysis (k = 91, N = 38,124): detachment correlated with less exhaustion, less fatigue, better sleep and more positive affect [PM-abs] — [Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2016, PMID 28133454](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02072)
- Exhaustion predicted *decreasing* detachment over 4 weeks (n = 109). The decline was strongest under time pressure and among those **without pleasurable leisure experiences** [PM-abs] — [Sonnentag et al. 2014, PMID 24635737](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035760)
- Day-level study (n = 166): low evening detachment predicted next-morning negative activation and fatigue. Relaxation predicted serenity, and mastery predicted positive activation [PM-abs] — [Sonnentag et al. 2008, PMID 18457495](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.93.3.674)
- Review: recovery mechanisms are similar across breaks, evenings and vacations, and appear enhanced in natural environments [PM-abs] — [Sonnentag et al. 2017, PMID 28358572](https://doi.org/10.1037/ocp0000079)

### Inferences
- For stressed + tired, rank options as follows: detach from the stressor (switch off work or notifications), relax (breathing, warm shower), take a 10-min nap if sleep-deprived, then take an easy walk for more than 20 min if the aim is less fatigue. A 10-min walk is for energy only.
- "Pleasurable leisure" is the recovery buffer, which supports adding a low-effort PLEASANT option to R10.

### Gaps
- No study tested napping specifically for *stress* plus fatigue. The nap trials measured sleepiness, fatigue and vigor after sleep restriction, not tension.
- Thayer's "tense-tiredness" construct (book, 1989/1996) has no intervention trial indexed in PubMed. The PubMed search for "Thayer tense tiredness" returned nothing.

## 3. Angry + sad / angry + stressed / angry + anxious

### Takeaway
Rumination is the shared risk factor for anger and sadness. Distraction beats rumination for anger-driven aggression. **Self-distanced reflection** (third-person, "why" from a distance) is tested separately on anger recall and on sadness in depressed adults, and it lowered negative affect in both. One new trial found that moderate aerobic exercise done *while* still exposed to aversive input *increased* anger and did not reduce anxiety.

### Cited Findings
- Recall of an anger-eliciting interpersonal experience: a "distanced-why" focus allowed reflection without reactivating "hot" negative affect, across 2 experiments, mediated by more abstract construal [PM-abs] — [Kross, Ayduk & Mischel 2005, PMID 16137257](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01600.x)
- Adults with major depressive disorder analysing a depressing experience: self-distancing lowered negative affect and depressotypic thought accessibility versus self-immersion and promoted closure **without avoidance**. It had no effect in non-depressed participants [PM-abs] — [Kross et al. 2012, PMID 22708885](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028808)
- Daily diary: reflecting on negative (and positive) events from a self-distanced perspective was associated with *shorter* emotional episodes [PM-abs] — [Verduyn et al. 2012, PMID 22642344](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028289)
- Provoked participants who ruminated for 25 min were more aggressive toward a fumbling confederate than distracted participants. Provocation-related negative affect predicted aggression *only* among ruminators (3 studies, up to 8 h delay) [PM-abs] — [Bushman et al. 2005, PMID 15982116](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.6.969)
- After provocation, rumination reduced self-control and increased aggression. State rumination, not anger itself, mediated trait rumination's effect (4 studies) [PM-abs] — [Denson et al. 2011, PMID 21421767](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211401420)
- Angry rumination temporarily reduces self-control and recruits regions for negative affect, arousal and self-reflection (multiple systems model; theory) [PM-abs] — [Denson 2013, PMID 23175519](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868312467086)
- Meta-analysis of anger-management activities (prior notes): arousal-decreasing activities reduced anger, arousal-increasing ones did not; venting does not help [PM-abs, carried from prior pass] — [Kjærvik & Bushman 2024, PMID 38518585](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102414)
- RCT with 52 women: 30 min of moderate cycling (64–76% HRmax) **while viewing aversive images** did not reduce state anxiety. It *increased* anger immediately after the session (time × group p = .028). There were no effects under neutral images [PM-abs] — [Rodrigues et al. 2026, PMID 41875642](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.106714)
- Daily life (two ESM studies, N = 46 and 95): rumination and suppression were associated with more NA and less PA, while reflection was associated with more PA. Distraction was the most used strategy [PM-abs] — [Brans et al. 2013, PMID 23731436](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032400)

### Inferences
- For angry + sad, R13's "expressive journalling" should be specified as **self-distanced** journalling (third person, "why did *they/you* feel…"), not free emotional writing, which risks rumination. Distraction is the acute first step.
- For angry + stressed or angry + anxious, keep movement easy and **away from the provoking input** (no exercising while scrolling the triggering thread).

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures distraction versus reappraisal for a state that is *simultaneously* angry and sad. PubMed hits were developmental (mother–child) or ageing studies.
- No RCT was found on exercise for "irritability" in non-clinical adults. Searches returned only PMS and training-overload studies.

## 4. Lonely + sad / lonely + anxious

### Takeaway
Loneliness interventions that measured depression and anxiety reduced all three. The best randomised evidence favours social-cognitive and acceptance components over contact alone. For socially anxious people, **kind acts** raised positive affect and lowered avoidance goals, while anxiety-reduction exercises did not raise positive affect. Nostalgia counteracts loneliness's effect on perceived support. Pet ownership is not associated with lower depression.

### Cited Findings
- RCT, n = 240, 4 weeks of layperson empathy-focused phone calls: loneliness (UCLA) d = 0.48, depression (PHQ) d = 0.31, anxiety (GAD-7) d = 0.35, mental health d = 0.46 [PM-abs] — [Kahlon et al. 2021, PMID 33620417](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0113)
- Psychological interventions for loneliness: 28 RCTs, N = 3,039, g = 0.43, with considerable heterogeneity; CBT was the most common [PM-abs] — [Hickin et al. 2021, PMID 34339939](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102066)
- In randomised studies, interventions addressing maladaptive social cognition were the most successful [PM-abs] — [Masi et al. 2011, PMID 20716644](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868310377394)
- Smartphone mindfulness dismantling RCT, n = 153, 14 lessons: monitoring plus **acceptance** reduced daily loneliness by 22% (d = 0.44) and added about two interactions a day (d = 0.47). Monitoring alone did not [PM-abs] — [Lindsay et al. 2019, PMID 30808743](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813588116)
- High socially anxious adults (n = 142, 4 weeks): kind acts increased positive affect, raised relationship satisfaction and decreased social-avoidance goals. Behavioural experiments aimed at reducing NA and activity monitoring did not raise PA [PM-abs] — [Alden & Trew 2013, PMID 22642341](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027761)
- Four studies: loneliness reduced perceived social support but increased nostalgia, and nostalgia in turn increased perceived support. The effect was strongest in resilient people [PM-abs] — [Zhou et al. 2008, PMID 19000213](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02194.x)
- 28 countries, N = 2,606: recalling a nostalgic (vs ordinary) memory increased social connectedness, self-continuity and meaning. Affect change was mild overall [PM-abs] — [Hepper et al. 2024, PMID 38252088](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001521)
- Pet ownership and depression (21 observational studies, n = 159,322): OR = 1.03 (0.995–1.07), not significant. Cat ownership OR = 1.06 (1.02–1.09); dog ownership OR = 0.93 (n.s.) [PM-abs] — [Moshfeghinia et al. 2025, PMID 41194144](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12991-025-00600-x)

### Inferences
- Lonely + sad: a warm one-to-one contact is the best-tested option, since Kahlon's calls hit loneliness and depression together. Nostalgia (look at photos of a valued shared time) is a no-contact option. Reminiscence on unrepeatable good times raises sadness as well (see §7), so frame it toward connection, not loss.
- Lonely + anxious: KIND (low-exposure kind act) and acceptance-based mindfulness have direct evidence in socially anxious or lonely samples. "Text before call" fits Alden & Trew's finding that PA enhancement, not fear reduction, lowered avoidance.
- Pets: do not suggest getting a pet as a fix. Interacting with an existing pet has no evidence here either way.

### Gaps
- The Kahlon trial was in homebound, chronically ill adults during COVID-19, so generalisation to students is untested.
- Social anxiety was not an outcome in any of the loneliness trials found.

## 5. Bored + restless / bored + lonely / bored + sad

### Takeaway
Direct intervention evidence for boredom combinations is essentially absent. The MAC model holds that boredom comes from an attention or meaning mismatch. Westgate's thinking-for-pleasure experiments show that reflective activity is enjoyable only if the topic is both **meaningful and positive**. Boredom frequency tracks depression closely in daily life.

### Cited Findings
- MAC model: boredom arises from a lack of attention (under- or over-challenge) and/or a lack of meaning (theory paper) [PM-abs, carried from prior pass] — [Westgate & Wilson 2018, PMID 29963873](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000097)
- Two preregistered studies: giving examples of meaningful topics made thinking for pleasure more enjoyable. Asking for "meaningful" thoughts did not, because some were negative. Topics must be meaningful *and* positive [PM-abs] — [Westgate et al. 2021, PMID 33661664](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000941)
- Daily diaries (n = 614): frequency of sadness and **boredom** were the emotions most strongly associated with depression [PM-abs] — [Eckland et al. 2022, PMID 34766792](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001038)
- High boredom-proneness scorers reported higher anxiety and depression (cross-sectional) [web] — [Sommers & Vodanovich 2000, PMID 10661377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10661377/)
- A web search found **no** meta-analysis of boredom proneness with depression or anxiety. Single studies report r ≈ 0.55–0.65 [web; single study, not verified] — see search note in Gaps

### Inferences
- Bored + sad: avoid "sit and reflect". Use behavioural activation (an activity that matches skill), or, if reflective, a guided *positive-meaningful* prompt. Treat persistent bored + sad as a depression signal for the safety gate's "persistent" check.
- Bored + restless and bored + lonely: no new evidence. They stay extrapolated.

### Gaps
- No RCT of any boredom-reduction activity was found in PubMed. The searches "boredom meaningful activity intervention" and "boredom loneliness" returned only cross-sectional or qualitative studies.

## 6. Anxious + energetic (excited + anxious; performance arousal)

### Takeaway
When anxiety comes with high arousal before a performance, reappraising the arousal as excitement or as useful ("get excited", "stress helps") beat trying to calm down, in several experiments and in a 339-student field trial. Pooled effects on performance are small (d ≈ 0.2). This contradicts R27's default of BREATHE for excited + anxious, at least in performance contexts.

### Cited Findings
- Most people believe calming down is the best way to cope with pre-performance anxiety. Across karaoke, public-speaking and maths studies, reappraising anxiety as excitement (saying "I am excited" out loud) increased felt excitement and an opportunity mindset and improved performance, compared with trying to calm down [PM-abs] — [Brooks 2014, PMID 24364682](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035325)
- Field RCT, N = 339 community-college students before an exam: stress reappraisal lowered maths evaluation anxiety and threat appraisals, produced more adaptive cortisol and testosterone responses, raised exam scores and increased course persistence [PM-abs] — [Jamieson et al. 2022, PMID 34292050](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000893)
- Meta-analysis of arousal reappraisal and stress-is-enhancing mindset RCTs (44 effects): performance d = 0.23. Combined interventions d = 0.45, reappraisal only d = 0.22, mindset only d = 0.18 (n.s.). The effect was larger for public performance (d = 0.34) [PM-abs] — [Bosshard & Gomez 2024, PMID 38575696](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-58408-w)
- Short film clips changed stress mindset. A stress-is-enhancing mindset was associated with moderate cortisol reactivity and greater desire for feedback [PM-abs] — [Crum et al. 2013, PMID 23437923](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031201)
- Integrated "stress optimisation" model, combining mindset with reappraisal (theory) [PM-abs] — [Crum et al. 2020, PMID 31961190](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000670); [Jamieson et al. 2018, PMID 29471669](https://doi.org/10.1080/10615806.2018.1442615)

### Inferences
- Route anxious + energetic with an upcoming task to a 1-minute "this energy is fuel, say 'I'm excited'" prompt, with breathing as the alternative. Without a task, R05's calming options remain reasonable.
- Outcomes in these studies were performance and appraisal. Felt anxiety fell in Jamieson 2022, but Brooks found more excitement rather than less anxiety.

### Gaps
- No arousal-reappraisal meta-analysis on *subjective anxiety* specifically was found in PubMed in this pass. Bosshard pooled performance only.

## 7. Positive–negative blends (bittersweet, nostalgia, content + tired, accepting a mixed state)

### Takeaway
Longitudinal and therapy studies associate mixed emotions with better outcomes later, and habitual *acceptance* of negative emotions predicts less negative emotion under stress and better health. This supports "don't force a single label, don't fix the positive part". Bittersweet states arise from reminiscence about unrepeatable good times.

### Cited Findings
- 10-year experience-sampling study across adulthood: frequent mixed emotions were associated with better physical health, and increases in mixed emotions attenuated age-related health decline [PM-abs] — [Hershfield et al. 2013, PMID 24032072](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550612444616)
- 47 adults in psychotherapy: concurrent happiness and sadness in narratives were associated with, and preceded, improvements in wellbeing beyond either emotion alone [PM-abs] — [Adler & Hershfield 2012, PMID 22539987](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0035633)
- Emodiversity (variety of emotions) predicted less depression and fewer doctor visits over and above mean PA and NA (two cross-sectional studies, more than 37,000 respondents) [PM-abs] — [Quoidbach et al. 2014, PMID 25285428](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025)
- Habitual acceptance of mental experiences predicted wellbeing, life satisfaction and lower depression and anxiety (n = 1,003). It predicted lower NA (not PA) to a lab stressor (n = 156) and lower NA in daily stressors, which explained health 6 months later (n = 222). The benefit was specific to accepting mental experiences, not situations [PM-abs] — [Ford et al. 2018, PMID 28703602](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000157)
- Reminiscing on good times elicited happiness, sadness and mixed emotions. Good times that were *not repeatable* produced especially intense sadness and mixed feelings (3 studies) [PM-abs] — [Larsen et al. 2021, PMID 34591508](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001011)
- Bittersweet stimuli consistently raise co-occurrence of happiness and sadness, and the PA–NA correlation is distinct from co-occurrence [PM-abs] — [Larsen et al. 2017, PMID 27709977](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000231)
- Daily responses to positive emotions centred on savouring, future focus and behavioural activation [PM-abs] — [Heiy & Cheavens 2014, PMID 24999913](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037231)
- After anxiety, contentment speeds recovery: positive states in a blend do regulatory work [PM-abs] — [Fredrickson et al. 2000, PMID 21731120](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1010796329158)

### Inferences
- When a positive class (content, calm, energetic) co-occurs with a negative one, the app should not aim to remove the positive component. Offer acceptance framing ("both can be true") plus an activity that keeps the positive part, such as savouring or sharing.
- Content + tired: no study tests savouring under fatigue. Savouring is low effort, so R26 stays plausible but extrapolated.

### Gaps
- All mixed-emotion benefit data are correlational or longitudinal. No trial *induced* mixed emotions as an intervention and measured later wellbeing.

## 8. General principles for several simultaneous emotions

### Takeaway
People routinely use many strategies per episode (about 15 across 10 days). Using **more** strategies helps only if they are the right ones (engagement, behavioural, reinterpretation, situation selection). Adding rumination or suppression makes it worse. Emotion differentiation (naming feelings separately) shows only negligible-to-small links with psychopathology in daily life. Big ESM data give limited support for the idea that people fit strategies to intensity or controllability. The "regulate the highest-arousal emotion first" ordering remains untested.

### Cited Findings
- ESM, 10 days, 40 strategies × 14 emotions: participants used about 15 different strategies for negative emotions (most often acceptance, behavioural activation and rumination) and about 16 for positive emotions. The most frequent strategies were often not the most effective [PM-abs] — [Heiy & Cheavens 2014, PMID 24999913](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037231)
- EMA, n = 92: more between-person Adaptive Engagement and within-person Adaptive Engagement, Enhancement and Behavioural strategies predicted better mood. More Aversive Cognitive and Disengagement strategies predicted worse mood ("more of the right strategies is better") [PM-abs] — [Southward & Cheavens 2020, PMID 32696710](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2020.1797637)
- ESM, n = 179, 21 days: wellbeing was highest for people using several profiles of *active* strategies and lowest for suppression-focused profiles [PM-abs] — [Grommisch et al. 2020, PMID 31478725](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000669)
- EMA, n = 106: higher event intensity predicted more strategies used. Within polyregulation, situation selection and reinterpretation predicted lower daily NA [PM-abs] — [Dicker et al. 2026, PMID 41806361](https://doi.org/10.1080/10615806.2026.2640041)
- Two EMA studies (US n = 158, China n = 144; more than 23,000 observations): polyregulation was linked to *higher* distress when few of the strategies were personally effective and lower distress when most were. Strategy count alone was not the driver [PM-abs] — [Liu et al. 2026, PMID 42756577](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42761-026-00398-1)
- Integrative ESM analysis (up to 11 datasets, more than 100,000 observations): **limited support** that intensity, controllability or social context shape everyday strategy use [PM-abs] — [Tran et al. 2026, PMID 42956182](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261470400)
- Diary (n = 614): pleasant and unpleasant emotion differentiation was associated with psychopathology, but effect sizes were "negligible to small" [PM-abs] — [Eckland et al. 2022, PMID 34766792](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001038)
- 4-year diary (N = 502): greater negative emotion differentiation was associated with less disengagement regulation (e.g. substance use). Most hypothesised moderation effects were non-significant [PM-abs] — [Brown et al. 2021, PMID 33829837](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000969)
- Emotion differentiation as a protective skill (review) [memory; DOI from prior notes] — [Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721414550708)
- Polyregulation concept (Ford, Gross & Gruber 2019, *Emotion Review*) [memory; not in PubMed, DOI not verified]
- Self-distancing shortens both negative and positive episodes [PM-abs] — [Verduyn et al. 2012, PMID 22642344](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028289)

### Inferences
- Offering a short *sequence* (calm the body, then one engagement step) is consistent with the evidence that "more right strategies" helps. Including rumination-like prompts ("think about why you feel this way" without distance) is the main risk.
- Personal effectiveness dominates (Liu 2026). The app's "did that help?" re-ranking (R29) is directly supported as the mechanism that makes multi-strategy suggestions beneficial.
- Asking users to label two feelings separately is cheap and plausibly helpful, but the evidence for differentiation as a lever is weak.

### Gaps
- No study was found testing whether regulating the most intense or highest-arousal emotion *first* improves outcomes when two emotions co-occur. R01's ordering remains an extrapolation from Sheppes et al. (2011).

## Rule review

- **R10 Stressed + tired — refine.** Recovery evidence supports detachment, relaxation and rest ([Karabinski 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/ocp0000280); [Sonnentag 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.93.3.674); [Albulescu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)). Three changes. (a) A short walk raises energy, but fatigue falls only after low-to-moderate exercise lasting more than 20 min ([Loy 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266), web), and in fatigued people only after *low* intensity ([Dishman 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2010.01014.x)). (b) If sleep-deprived, nap 10–20 min, not 30 ([Brooks & Lack 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831)). (c) Add a low-effort pleasurable activity, because exhaustion erodes detachment unless pleasurable leisure is present ([Sonnentag 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035760)).
- **R11 Stressed + anxious — refine.** Mindfulness self-help helps stress more than anxiety ([Taylor 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102078)), consistent with the rule. When the stress is about an upcoming evaluated task, add arousal reappraisal ("this energy helps me") as an option. It reduced evaluation anxiety and threat in a 339-person field RCT ([Jamieson 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000893)).
- **R12 Anxious + lonely — confirm, with additions.** Kind acts raised PA and reduced avoidance in socially anxious adults ([Alden & Trew 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027761)). One-to-one empathic calls cut loneliness and anxiety ([Kahlon 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0113)). Add acceptance-based mindfulness ([Lindsay 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813588116)).
- **R13 Angry + sad — refine.** Specify REAPPRAISE and journalling as *self-distanced* reflection, which is tested on anger recall ([Kross 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01600.x)) and on sadness in depression ([Kross 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028808)). Distraction before reflection, since rumination raises aggression ([Bushman 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.6.969)). Undirected "expressive" writing should be dropped.
- **R14 Angry + stressed — confirm.** New RCT: moderate aerobic exercise done amid aversive input increased anger ([Rodrigues 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.106714)). Keep movement easy and away from the trigger, and use distraction over rumination ([Bushman 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.6.969)).
- **R15 Sad + tired — refine.** Use a walk for energy, but expect fatigue relief only from easy movement lasting more than 20 min ([Loy 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266), web). BA's evidence is for depression ([Jia 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)). Chronic-exercise energy effects vanished against placebo controls ([Puetz 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.866)), so keep the copy modest.
- **R16 Sad + lonely — confirm, with additions.** Directly supported by [Kahlon 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0113) (loneliness d = 0.48, depression d = 0.31). Add nostalgia as a no-contact option ([Zhou 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02194.x)), framed toward connection, not loss ([Larsen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001011)). Do not suggest pets ([Moshfeghinia 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12991-025-00600-x)).
- **R17 Anxious + sad — refine.** Physical activity, UP-style emotion regulation and mindfulness reduce both outcomes ([Singh 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106195); [Carlucci 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.101999); [Taylor 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102078)). Digital BA reduced depression but not anxiety ([Jia 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)), so PLEASANT should be ranked after MOVE/MINDFUL. Single-bout anxiety effects are small ([Ensari 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.22370)).
- **R18 Bored + restless — no new evidence.** No intervention trial was found. The rule rests on the MAC model only ([Westgate & Wilson 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000097)).
- **R19 Bored + lonely — no new evidence.**
- **R20 Bored + tired — refine (nap length).** A 10-min nap is most recuperative and 30 min causes sleep inertia ([Brooks & Lack 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831); [Tietzel & Lack 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/24.3.293)). The rest of the rule is unchanged.
- **R21 Anxious + tired — confirm (indirect).** Evening relaxation predicts serenity and detachment predicts less fatigue ([Sonnentag 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.93.3.674)). Low-intensity movement lowers fatigue in the fatigued ([Dishman 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2010.01014.x)). No combination trial.
- **R22 Stressed + sad — confirm.** Physical activity has its largest effect on distress (−0.60; [Singh 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106195)), BA also lowered stress ([Jia 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)), and combined strategies are rated best ([Thayer 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-3514.67.5.910)).
- **R23 Stressed + lonely — no new evidence.** Kahlon did not measure stress. The rule remains extrapolated.
- **R24 Happy + energetic — no new evidence.**
- **R25 Calm + content — no new evidence.** Savouring is the dominant daily response to positive emotion ([Heiy & Cheavens 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037231)), but that is descriptive, not efficacy.
- **R26 Happy + tired — no new evidence.** Short rest and relaxation are supported for tiredness ([Albulescu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)), but nothing tests the blend.
- **R27 Mixed valence — refine and partly contradict.** Not suppressing either feeling and accepting the mixed state are supported ([Hershfield 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550612444616); [Adler & Hershfield 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0035633); [Ford 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000157)). "Naming both feelings" has only weak support ([Eckland 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001038); [Brown 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000969)). For excited + anxious before a task, the evidence **contradicts** calming as the default: reappraising arousal as excitement outperformed trying to calm down ([Brooks 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035325); meta-analytic d = 0.23, [Bosshard & Gomez 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-58408-w)). Offer "get excited" self-talk first and breathing as an alternative.
