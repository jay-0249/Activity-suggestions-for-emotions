# Same-sample multi-emotion effects of physical, nature and mind-body activities

Scope: studies in which one activity was measured on several mood dimensions in the same participants (POMS, PANAS, STAI with PSS, DASS-21, energy/fatigue scales), so that combination rules (e.g. stressed+tired, anxious+sad) can rest on joint outcomes rather than on composed single-emotion findings. Verification tags: [PM-abs] = read in the PubMed abstract this session; [PM-full] = read in PMC full text this session; [prior-notes] = taken from the earlier notes in `research_notes/Emotion to activity evidence map/physical_nature_activities.md`, which were checked against abstracts in that pass; [memory] = from background knowledge, not re-checked here (treat as unverified). Searches were run through NCBI E-utilities (3 batches, about 110 queries) plus PMC full-text pulls for 11 papers; no publisher pages were opened.

Mapping used throughout: POMS tension-anxiety → **anxious** (for brief, non-clinical sessions "stressed/tense" is often the closer everyday reading); depression-dejection → **sad**; anger-hostility → **angry**; fatigue-inertia → **tired**; vigor-activity → **energetic**; PSS and DASS stress → **stressed**; STAI-state → **anxious**; PANAS positive affect (active, alert, enthusiastic items) → listed under **energetic** with the label "PA", because PANAS-PA is activated positive affect, not contentment; PANAS negative affect (NA) goes under other_dimensions. Almost no study measured *calm* or *content* directly. A few semantic-differential "relaxed" ratings are recorded under calm.

## 1. Per-activity same-sample effects: which dimensions moved together and which did not

### Takeaway
Forest and urban-green walks are the only activity class with many same-sample, all-six-POMS-subscale datasets. Brief (≈15 min) forest walks or viewing in Japanese young adults usually improve tension, depression, anger, fatigue, confusion and vigor together. Longer and day-long programmes sometimes move the negative subscales but not vigor (Furuyashiki 2019) or not anger (Li 2016). Acute aerobic exercise reliably raises vigor/energy but often fails to lower fatigue, and in some trials matches quiet rest on the negative subscales (Bartholomew 2005; Loy 2013). Yoga, tai chi, slow breathing, mindfulness and PMR improve several distress dimensions together. PMR's benefit is concentrated on stress and anxiety, with depression often unchanged. Naps and micro-breaks act jointly on fatigue and vigor, with little evidence on the negative-affect dimensions.

### Cited Findings

**Forest bathing / shinrin-yoku**
- 19 forest vs urban sites, 228 male students, 15 min of seated viewing: POMS vigor significantly higher, and tension-anxiety, depression-dejection, anger-hostility, fatigue and confusion all significantly lower in forest vs urban; "refreshed" rating higher [PM-abs] — [19-forest study (Takayama et al.? author unverified) 2011, Nihon Eiseigaku Zasshi, PMID 21996766](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21996766/)
- 585 young adults (mean age 21.7), 15-min forest vs city walk: all five negative subscales ↓ and vigor ↑ [PM-abs]. Post-walk T-scores forest vs city: T-A 35.5±5.5 vs 40.4±7.7; A-H 38.1±3.9 vs 39.5±4.7; F 37.5±6.2 vs 42.7±8.4; C 39.8±5.6 vs 42.2±6.7; all p<0.01 (Wilcoxon) [PM-full]. Note how small the anger change is (≈1.4 T-points) next to fatigue (≈5.2) and tension (≈4.9) — [Song et al. 2018, IJERPH 15:2804, PMID 30544682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30544682/)
- 60 young women, 6 forests vs 6 city areas, ≈15-min walk: vigor ↑; T-A, D-D, A-H, F, C ↓; TMD ↓; STAI-state ↓; all p<0.01 except depression-dejection (p<0.05) [PM-abs; PM-full] — [Song et al. 2019, IJERPH 16:229, PMID 30650572](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30650572/)
- 155 working-age adults, day-long forest-bathing session (≈2 h of slow walking): all five negative subscales ↓ (p=0.001 to <0.001); TMD went from 5.8±12.7 to −4.6±6.3. **Vigor did not change** [PM-full]. People with depressive tendencies (K6 5–12; 37%) improved more — [Furuyashiki et al. 2019, Environ Health Prev Med 24:46, PMID 31228960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31228960/)
- 19 middle-aged men, 2×80-min forest walks (2.6 km each) vs urban walk: vigor ↑, and anxiety, depression, fatigue and confusion ↓. **Anger was unaffected by either trip** ("data not shown"). Urban walking *raised* fatigue and *lowered* vigor [PM-full] — [Li et al. 2016, Evid Based Complement Alternat Med, PMID 27493670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27493670/)
- 20 healthy men (57±8 y), forest programme (2.5 km for 2 h, morning and afternoon) vs urban trip: serum serotonin ↑; vigor ↑ and fatigue ↓ in POMS. The abstract reports no other subscale as changed; sleepiness on rising and recovery from fatigue improved [PM-abs] — [Li et al. 2022, Environ Health Prev Med 27:44, PMID 36328588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36328588/)
- 31 women (40±2 y) with depression or depressive tendencies, randomized crossover, forest vs city (2×90 min walks): SDS depression ↓ vs city and baseline, still lower 1 week later; POMS positive feelings ↑ and negative emotions ↓ vs city; subjective fatigue ↓; sleep better [PM-abs] — [Li et al. 2025, Diseases 13:100, PMID 40277811](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40277811/)
- 30 men (63±8 y) at risk of COPD, randomized crossover forest vs city day trips: POMS positive ↑ and negative ↓, subjective fatigue ↓, sleep better, CRP/IL-6 ↓ [PM-abs] — [Li et al. 2025, J Occup Health, PMID 40673365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40673365/)
- 24 elderly hypertensive patients, 7-day forest vs city stay (randomized to groups): "scores in the negative subscales were lowered" after forest; BP and RAS markers ↓ [PM-abs]. Vigor not reported in the abstract — [Mao et al. 2012 (author [memory]), J Cardiol, PMID 22948092](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22948092/)
- 498 healthy volunteers, forest day vs control day, Multiple Mood Scale plus STAI: hostility (p<0.001) and depression (p<0.001) ↓ and liveliness ↑ (p=0.001) on the forest day; environment main effect on all outcomes except hostility. The higher the stress level, the greater the effect [PM-abs] — [Morita et al. 2007, Public Health 121:54, PMID 17055544](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17055544/)
- 47 young men, stay-in forest therapy at 4 sites, crossover: forest "psychologically relaxing and enhanced psychological vigor" (POMS) [PM-abs; subscale detail not in abstract] — [PMID 24858504](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24858504/)
- Young Japanese men, 3-day field experiment: positive feelings ↑ and negative feelings ↓ vs urban; salivary cortisol ↓ [PM-abs] — [Lee et al. 2011, Public Health 125:93, PMID 21288543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21288543/) (corrigendum PMID 30979383)
- **Subscale meta-analysis (forest therapy, POMS):** studies pooled with WMD. For sessions ≥60 min, all six subscales significant: T −1.794 (−2.513 to −1.075); D −1.420 (−1.860 to −0.980); A −1.674 (−2.492 to −0.855); F −2.129 (−3.058 to −1.200); C −1.181; V +1.996 (1.408 to 2.584); all p≤0.002. **For 15–60 min sessions, fatigue (−0.131, p=0.68) and vigor (+0.091, p=0.58) were not significant**, while T, D, A and C were small but significant. For ≤15 min, only C and V were significant (T, D, A, F ns). European samples: F (p=0.38) and V (p=0.14) ns and A borderline (p=0.055); Asian samples: all six significant [PM-full]. Caveats: Table 4 has internal inconsistencies (e.g. dynamic-anger WMD −0.026 with CI −1.647 to −0.405; static-T CI excludes zero but p=0.064), and Egger p=0.001 for the T scale (publication bias) [PM-full] — [Front Psychol 2025;16:1670804, PMID 41256089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41256089/)
- **Thermal-condition meta-analysis:** 9 studies (30 cases). Under thermally neutral conditions, forest exposure improved POMS tension SMD −1.36 (−2.08 to −0.64), fatigue −0.90 (−1.24 to −0.57), confusion −1.10 and vigor +1.23 (0.37 to 2.09). Effects were inconsistent in cold or hot conditions. Certainty was moderate for the psychological outcomes [PM-abs] — [Sci Rep 2025;15:39222, PMID 41214175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41214175/)
- Earlier forest meta-analysis (Kotera, Richardson & Sheffield 2022) reported short-term reductions in anxiety, anger, depression and stress, but its pooled effect sizes were not verified (journal not in PubMed) [prior-notes] — [doi 10.1007/s11469-020-00363-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-020-00363-4)

**Urban green walks**
- 17 young men, 15-min spring urban-park vs city-street walk: vigor ↑, tension-anxiety ↓, fatigue ↓, STAI-state ↓. **"No significant differences in the scores for depression, anger-hostility and confusion"** [PM-full] — [Song et al. 2014, J Physiol Anthropol 33:8, PMID 24887352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24887352/)
- 23 young men, 15-min fall urban-park walk: tension-anxiety, anger-hostility, fatigue and confusion significantly lower than after the city walk; **depression-dejection only marginal**; more "relaxed" and "vigorous"; STAI ↓ [PM-full] — [Song et al. 2015, IJERPH 12:14216, PMID 26569271](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26569271/)
- 13 young men, 15-min winter urban-park walk: improved mood, fewer negative feelings, lower anxiety (POMS, STAI) [PM-abs; subscales not given] — [Song et al. 2013, J Physiol Anthropol 32:18, PMID 24168929](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24168929/)
- 48 middle-aged and older adults in Chengdu, 1.6-km walk: daytime green walk improved PANAS/POMS/restoration and lowered BP; daytime urban walk slightly worsened all parameters (ns); at night, green and urban walks did not differ [PM-abs] — [IJERPH 2020;18:90, PMID 33374368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33374368/)
- Green-exercise multi-study analysis (10 UK studies, 1,252 participants): mood d=0.54 (0.38–0.69) and self-esteem d=0.46. The largest gains came from short engagements; water added to the effect [PM-abs] — [Barton & Pretty 2010, Environ Sci Technol, PMID 20337470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337470/)

**Gardening**
- 30 allotment gardeners, stressful Stroop task, then 30 min gardening vs indoor reading: cortisol fell more after gardening; positive mood was fully restored after gardening but deteriorated further during reading [PM-abs] — [Van den Berg & Custers 2011, J Health Psychol, PMID 20522508](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20522508/)
- 27 older adults (60–76 y), four short horticultural activities: POMS improved significantly only for Rocky Leaf Prints and Herb Tasting and Smelling; salivary amylase ↓; authors cite relaxation, stress reduction and vigor enhancement [PM-abs; subscale table not read] — [IJERPH 2020;17:5225, PMID 32698351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32698351/)
- Gardening meta-analysis, 22 case studies with 76 comparisons: positive effect on health outcomes including depression, anxiety and life satisfaction. No subscale-level emotion pooling [PM-abs] — [Soga et al. 2017, Prev Med Rep 5:92, PMID 27981022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27981022/)

**Acute aerobic exercise / brisk walking**
- Patients with MDD, 30 min treadmill at 60–70% HRmax vs 30 min quiet rest: **both** reduced distress, depression, confusion, fatigue, tension and anger similarly; **only exercise** raised vigor and positive well-being [PM-abs]. n=40 [memory] — [Bartholomew, Morrison & Ciccolo 2005, Med Sci Sports Exerc 37:2032, PMID 16331126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16331126/)
- 24 women with MDD, 30 min cycling at light, moderate or hard intensity vs quiet rest: POMS depression improved acutely after exercise regardless of intensity (p>0.05 between intensities) [PM-abs] — [Meyer et al. 2016, Psychoneuroendocrinology, PMID 27697714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27697714/)
- 24 people with MS, 20 min treadmill walking vs yoga vs quiet rest (counterbalanced): condition×time on TMD (ηp²=0.13), with walking ≈ yoga; condition×time on vigor (ηp²=0.23) — **walking raised vigor, yoga did not**. Time main effect for anger, confusion, depression and tension (p<0.05), **but not for fatigue** [PM-abs] — [Ensari, Sandroff & Motl 2016, Int J MS Care 18:1, PMID 26917992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26917992/)
- 22 women (45 y), maximal walking test, abbreviated POMS: tension ↓ and self-esteem ↑, but **fatigue ↑ and vigor ↓** after maximal exertion [PM-abs] — [Physiol Behav 1995;57:1, PMID 7878100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7878100/)
- 20 novice Taekwondo students vs 20 lecture controls, one 75-min session: tension, depression, anger, fatigue, confusion all improved and vigor ↑ vs control (p<0.007); controls got worse [PM-abs] — [Toskovic 2001 (author [memory]), Percept Mot Skills, PMID 11565911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11565911/)
- 8 CrossFit competitors, workouts >93% HRmax: significant week×time interactions for TMD, energy index and fatigue; mood rose to pre-workout levels and beyond by 60 min post [PM-abs] — [Sports 2018;6:67, PMID 30041416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30041416/)
- 10-min brisk walk vs candy bar, n=18: walking raised energy and lowered tension for up to 2 h [prior-notes] — [Thayer 1987, J Pers Soc Psychol, PMID 3820066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3820066/)
- Single-bout meta-analysis of energy vs fatigue, 16 studies, 678 participants: energy Δ=0.47 (0.39–0.56); **fatigue Δ=0.03 (−0.08 to 0.13), ns**. Fatigue fell only after >20 min of low-to-moderate intensity [prior-notes; journal not in PubMed] — [Loy, O'Connor & Dishman 2013, Fatigue: Biomed Health Behav](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266)
- Acute aerobic exercise → positive activated affect: 158 studies, mean corrected effect 0.47 [prior-notes; secondary citation only] — [Reed & Ones 2006](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-acute-aerobic-exercise-on-positive-activated-affect/)
- 30 sedentary adults, 6-h sitting days: a 30-min morning walk and 6×5-min hourly microbouts both raised energy and vigor; **only microbouts** improved mood and reduced fatigue at the end of the day [prior-notes] — [Bergouignan et al. 2016, PMID 27809874](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27809874/)
- 18 college women with chronic insufficient sleep: 10 min of stair walking raised POMS vigor more than 50 mg caffeine or placebo; other interactions ns [prior-notes] — [Randolph & O'Connor 2017, PMID 28302573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28302573/)
- Chronic exercise → energy and fatigue combined: mean δ=0.37; no effect in placebo-controlled trials of exercise alone [PM-abs] — [Puetz, O'Connor & Dishman 2006, Psychol Bull 132:866, PMID 17073524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073524/)

**Resistance exercise**
- 62 young adults, acute bout of moderate resistance exercise (2×8–12 reps, 8 exercises) vs quiet rest: no group×time interaction for POMS-B depression (p=0.198). Both reduced depressed mood (exercise d=0.29–1.26; rest d=0.27–0.39), including in subgroups with analogue GAD, analogue MDD and **comorbid AGAD+AMDD** [PM-abs] — [MSSE 2025;57:2496, PMID 40440519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440519/)
- 37 young women (trained and untrained), crossover high-intensity resistance bout vs rest: vigor ↑ immediately and at 30 min post (η²=0.172, p=0.001). Other subscales not reported as changed in the abstract [PM-abs] — [Physiol Behav 2026;303:115134, PMID 41110493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41110493/)
- 104 weight-training students, 5 intensity/rest conditions: positive affect higher only in the low-intensity, long-rest condition vs control at 5 min; anxiety peaked at 5 min post and fell below baseline at 20 and 40 min [PM-abs] — [Bibeau et al. 2010, J Strength Cond Res, PMID 19834350](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19834350/)
- Programme meta-analyses (different trial sets, so not same-sample): anxiety Δ=0.31 (0.17–0.44), 16 articles; depressive symptoms Δ=0.66 (0.48–0.83), 33 RCTs [PM-abs] — [Gordon et al. 2017, PMID 28819746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28819746/); [Gordon et al. 2018, JAMA Psychiatry, PMID 29800984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29800984/)
- Network meta-analysis of acute exercise for anxiety in youth, 33 studies: mind-body exercise SMD −0.36 (−0.70 to −0.03); aerobic −0.16 (−0.28 to −0.03); **resistance −0.09 (−0.33 to 0.14), ns** [PM-abs] — [Scand J Med Sci Sports 2024, PMID 39317982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39317982/)

**Yoga**
- 253 adult women, one 60-min Hatha session: tension-anxiety, depression, anger, fatigue and confusion ↓; vigor ↑; vigor gain larger at ≥41 years [PM-abs; no effect sizes in abstract] — [Healthcare 2026;14:1122, PMID 42121565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42121565/)
- 69 students, one 90-min class of Hatha yoga (n=18), African dance (n=21) or a lecture (n=30): PSS and PANAS-NA fell in both yoga and dance (interactions p<.0001); **PA rose only with dance**, not yoga; cortisol fell with yoga and rose with dance [PM-abs] — [West et al. 2004, Ann Behav Med 28:114, PMID 15454358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15454358/)
- 147 female teachers, one class each: state anxiety, depressive mood and well-being improved after yoga, Feldenkrais and swimming, **but not after aerobic dance** or a computer class [PM-abs] — [Netz & Lidor 2003, J Psychol, PMID 14629072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14629072/)
- 12 weeks of yoga vs metabolically matched walking, 60 min 3×/week, n=19 vs 15: greater improvement in mood and greater fall in anxiety with yoga; thalamic GABA correlated [PM-abs] — [Streeter et al. 2010, J Altern Complement Med, PMID 20722471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20722471/)
- 15 CFS patients, one session of sitting isometric yoga (after 8 weeks of practice): POMS fatigue ↓ (p<0.01) and vigor ↑ (p<0.01); cortisol ↓ [PM-abs] — [Biopsychosoc Med 2018, PMID 29643935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29643935/)
- Kripalu yoga for police-academy trainees (pilot, pre-post): perceived stress, tension and fatigue ↓; **anger only a trend** [PM-abs] — [Int J Yoga Therap 2013, PMID 24016821](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24016821/)
- High-school Kripalu yoga vs PE: TMD (p=.015), POMS tension-anxiety (p=.002) and NA (p=.006) improved relative to controls; **PA unchanged** [PM-abs] — [Noggle et al. 2012 (author [memory]), J Dev Behav Pediatr, PMID 22343481](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22343481/)
- 6-week workplace yoga, female teachers with chronic pain (RCT): DASS anxiety, depression and stress, plus sleep and fatigue, all improved vs control [PM-abs] — [Work 2023, PMID 36847050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36847050/)
- 8-week yoga, 120 adolescents with internet gaming disorder (RCT): DASS depression, anxiety and stress, plus UCLA loneliness, all improved vs control (p<0.001) [PM-abs] — [Front Public Health 2026, PMID 41846846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41846846/)
- 128 blue-collar workers, 1 month of workplace yoga: fatigue (Chalder) and burnout ↓ vs waitlist (p<0.001) [PM-abs] — [J Educ Health Promot 2024, PMID 38784274](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38784274/)
- 22 white-collar workers, 8-week tele-yoga, synchronous vs asynchronous: fatigue (FSS) fell in both groups; **PSS stress fell only in the synchronous group** [PM-abs] — [Altern Ther Health Med 2025, PMID 40293854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40293854/)
- 55 female students with binge eating, 8 weeks: DASS-21 improved in all groups with no group difference between LV-HIIT, HV-HIIT and yoga [PM-abs] — [Nutrients 2026, PMID 42451180](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42451180/)

**Tai chi / qigong**
- 96 tai chi practitioners randomized after mental-arithmetic and film stressors to tai chi, brisk walking, meditation or neutral reading: cortisol fell and mood improved after all four; tai chi beat reading on state anxiety and vigor, partly through expectancy [PM-abs] — [Jin 1992, J Psychosom Res 36:361, PMID 1593511](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1593511/)
- 39 older women, 24 weeks of tai chi vs control: fatigue −18.8%, depression −24.7%, vigour +18.3%, PSQI −24.6% at week 24. Only the depression and sleep gains persisted 4 weeks after stopping [PM-abs] — [Res Sports Med 2021, PMID 33401978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33401978/)
- Tai chi meta-analysis, 33 trials: Hedges g 0.66 stress, 0.66 anxiety, 0.56 depression, 0.45 mood (pooled across studies, not same-sample) [prior-notes] — [Wang et al. 2010, PMID 20492638](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20492638/)
- Baduanjin in university students, 35 RCTs (n=2,846): SDS depression, SAS anxiety, POMS negative mood, PSS stress and FS-14 fatigue all improved, plus sleep. Sleep and stress gains were not sustained after stopping [PM-abs] — [Front Psychol 2026, PMID 42220380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42220380/)
- Tai chi/qigong in adolescents, 10 studies (n=1,244): anxiety SMD 0.386 and depression 1.937 improved; **stress, mood and self-esteem ns** [PM-abs] — [Front Psychol 2021, PMID 34899487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34899487/)

**Dance**
- Argentine tango vs mindfulness meditation vs waitlist (66 completers): depression fell with tango (d=0.50) and meditation (d=0.54); **stress fell only with tango** (d=0.45) [PM-abs] — [Pinniger et al. 2012, Complement Ther Med, PMID 23131367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23131367/)
- African dance single class: PSS ↓, NA ↓, PA ↑ (see West 2004 above) [PM-abs]
- Aerobic dance single class in teachers: no mood improvement (see Netz & Lidor 2003) [PM-abs]
- 16 students, single Zumba sessions at low vs moderate intensity: PANAS-X PA ↑ and NA ↓ after exercise, more so at moderate intensity [PM-abs] — [Percept Mot Skills 2021, PMID 34120521](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34120521/)
- One Zumba exergame session, healthy young women: state anxiety ↓ (ES 0.62); enjoyment not correlated with the anxiety change [PM-abs] — [Games Health J 2017, PMID 29077484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29077484/)

**Slow breathing / breathwork**
- Remote RCT, 108 enrolled, 5 min/day for 28 days of cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation or mindfulness meditation: all groups showed daily PANAS-PA ↑, PANAS-NA ↓ and STAI-state ↓. Mean daily NA change −1.62±1.91 (mindfulness) vs −0.98±1.39 (all breathwork; −1.48±1.69 for cyclic sighing); mean daily state-anxiety change −3.95±4.16 (mindfulness) vs −3.03±3.83 (breathwork). Breathwork raised PA more than mindfulness, with cyclic sighing largest, and lowered respiratory rate more [PM-full] — [Balban et al. 2023, Cell Rep Med 4:100895, PMID 36630953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)
- Breathwork RCT meta-analysis: stress g=−0.35 (−0.55 to −0.14, k=12); anxiety g=−0.32 (k=20); depressive symptoms g=−0.40 (k=18). Pooled from different trial sets [PM-abs] — [Fincham et al. 2023, Sci Rep 13:432, PMID 36624160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36624160/)
- 40 adults, 20 sessions over 8 weeks of diaphragmatic breathing at 4 breaths/min: NA ↓, sustained attention ↑, cortisol ↓ [PM-abs] — [Ma et al. 2017 (author [memory]), Front Psychol, PMID 28626434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28626434/)
- 84 midlife women with high stress **and** high depressive symptoms, 3 weeks of Wim Hof Method (fast breathing plus cold showers) vs slow breathing plus warm showers: both groups improved, with depression −24%, anxiety −27% and stress −20%, maintained at 3 months. WHM reduced rumination after daily stressors [PM-abs] — [Compr Psychoneuroendocrinol 2024, PMID 39606690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39606690/)
- 141 women with high perceived stress, 4 three-week arms (mindfulness, slow breathing plus warm showers, WHM, HIIT): all arms reduced depressive symptoms (−15.9%) and perceived stress (−9.7%) with no group differences; WHM showed greater daily PA gains [PM-abs] — [Ann Behav Med 2025, PMID 41309213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41309213/)

**Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR)**
- 155 prostate-cancer patients (quasi-experimental), applied PMR: DASS anxiety (partial η²=0.198) and stress (0.103) ↓; **depression not improved (p=0.956)** [PM-abs] — [Asian Pac J Cancer Prev 2013, PMID 23725119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23725119/)
- Male auto assembly-line workers (quasi-experimental), PMR vs pamphlets: DASS stress ↓ (ES 0.6 vs 0.2; group×time p<0.001); **depression and anxiety minimal at baseline, little or no change** [PM-abs] — [Ind Health 2016, PMID 26726829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26726829/)
- 66 pregnant women with mild-to-moderate DASS scores (RCT), PMR plus guided imagery over 6 sessions: stress, anxiety and depression all ↓ (p<0.05); no change in controls [PM-abs] — [J Educ Health Promot 2018, PMID 29619392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29619392/)
- 44 earthquake survivors with PTSD (RCT), 6 weeks of tele-PMR: DASS depression ηp²=0.435, anxiety 0.521, stress 0.504; fatigue (FSS) 0.477; sleep 0.632 vs control [PM-abs] — [BMC Psychiatry 2026, PMID 42907791](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907791/)

**Mindfulness / brief meditation and loving-kindness**
- Four sessions of brief mindfulness training vs a recorded book: both improved mood, but **only meditation** reduced fatigue and anxiety [PM-abs] — [Zeidan et al. 2010, Conscious Cogn 19:597, PMID 20363650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20363650/)
- Brief vs sham mindfulness meditation vs control: real meditation reduced negative mood, depression, fatigue and confusion (and heart rate) more than sham or control. Tension and anger are not listed among the improved outcomes in the abstract [PM-abs] — [Zeidan et al. 2010, J Altern Complement Med 16:867, PMID 20666590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20666590/)
- Mindfulness arm of Balban 2023: PA ↑, NA ↓, state anxiety ↓ daily (above) [PM-full]
- 156 undergraduates, brief breath awareness vs loving-kindness vs gratitude vs control: LKM produced greater reductions in *implicit* NA than control (d=0.59); gratitude raised PA vs breath awareness (d=0.58) but increased reactivity to a cold-pressor stressor [PM-abs] — [PLoS One 2018, PMID 30540772](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30540772/)
- A few minutes of LKM vs matched control: social connection and positivity toward strangers rose on explicit and implicit measures [PM-abs] — [Hutcherson, Seppala & Gross 2008 (authors [memory]), Emotion, PMID 18837623](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18837623/)

**Naps**
- After nocturnal sleep restriction, 5, 10, 20 and 30 min naps vs no nap: the 10-min nap gave immediate improvements in sleepiness, **fatigue and vigor** and cognition, lasting up to 155 min; the 30-min nap caused sleep inertia first [PM-abs] — [Brooks & Lack 2006, Sleep 29:831, PMID 16796222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16796222/)
- 14 active students after partial sleep deprivation, 30-min nap vs no nap: POMS anxiety, fatigue, confusion and depression lower and vigour higher (anger not reported) [PM-abs] — [J Sports Sci 2020, PMID 32734824](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32734824/)
- 10 healthy elderly habitual nappers, 30-min afternoon nap: subjective sleepiness and fatigue ↓ [PM-abs] — [Tamaki et al. 2000 (author [memory]), Sleep Res Online, PMID 11382911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11382911/)

**Micro-breaks**
- Meta-analysis, 22 samples (N=2,335): vigor d=.36 (k=9, n=913) and fatigue reduction d=.35 (k=9, n=803); performance ns (d=.16) [PM-abs] — [Albulescu et al. 2022, PLoS One, PMID 36044424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/)
- Daily diary study of employees: micro-breaks went with lower fatigue and higher vigor at day's end [PM-abs] — [Psychol Rep 2025, PMID 39882696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39882696/)

**Cold exposure**
- 4 months of winter swimming vs controls: tension and fatigue ↓, vigour-activity ↑ (p<0.05) [PM-abs] — [Huttunen et al. 2004, Int J Circumpolar Health, PMID 15253480](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15253480/)
- 5-min head-out immersion at 20 °C: PANAS PA ↑ (more active, alert, attentive, proud, inspired) and NA ↓ (distress, nervousness); the two changes occurred independently [PM-abs] — [Yankouskaya et al. 2023, Biology 12:211, PMID 36829490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36829490/)
- Cold-water immersion meta-analysis, 11 RCTs: stress ↓ only at 12 h post (SMD −1.00); ns immediately, at 1 h, 24 h and 48 h; inflammation ↑ acutely [PM-abs] — [Cain et al. 2025, PLoS One, PMID 39879231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879231/)
- Cold-water exposure meta-analysis, 13 studies (n=4,105): estimates favoured cold water for depression, stress, wellbeing and mood but **anxiety showed little consistent benefit**; effects attenuated in controlled-only analyses; mostly high risk of bias [PM-abs] — [Front Physiol 2026, PMID 42819812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42819812/)

### Inferences
- **Pairs best supported by same-sample data:** anxious+tired, anxious+sad, angry+anxious and sad+tired for forest or green walks, Hatha yoga and Taekwondo-style vigorous classes. In these the POMS negative subscales and vigor moved together in one sample. Forest walks are the only class with dozens of replications and a subscale-level meta-analysis.
- **Dose matters for the tired/energetic dimensions:** in the forest POMS meta-analysis, fatigue and vigor needed ≥60 min (15–60 min sessions ns on both), while tension, depression and anger moved at shorter durations. For a brief nudge, nature helps the tense-negative dimensions more reliably than tiredness.
- **Exercise fixes "low energy" more reliably than "tired":** energy and vigor rise after single bouts (Reed & Ones; Loy energy Δ 0.47), but fatigue usually does not (Loy Δ 0.03) and rises after maximal efforts (PMID 7878100). In MDD, quiet rest matched exercise on all negative subscales (Bartholomew 2005), and in the resistance-exercise RCT, rest matched exercise for depressed mood (PMID 40440519). For stressed+tired, the case for gentle movement over rest is weak, and rest is itself an evidence-based option.
- **Yoga vs dance split by arousal:** in the same lab session, yoga lowered stress, NA and cortisol without raising PA, whereas African dance lowered stress and NA *and* raised PA, with cortisol rising (West 2004). Yoga suits stressed+anxious (calming); dance suits stressed+sad or stressed+bored (activation plus distress relief).
- **PMR is a stress/anxiety tool, not a sadness tool:** two of four PMR studies found depression unchanged while stress and/or anxiety improved. For anxious+sad, PMR alone is a weaker match than walking or yoga.
- **Anger is the least responsive dimension in nature studies:** it was unaffected in Li 2016, ns in Song 2014, had the smallest change in Song 2018, was borderline in the European subgroup and had no environment main effect in Morita 2007. Rules that rely on nature for angry+X should state that the angry component is the weakest-supported part.
- Single-number composites (TMD, PANAS-NA, "negative POMS") hide which dimensions moved. Several rows below therefore cannot fill the specific columns.

### Gaps
- No study found that measured *calm* or *content* as such; the closest are semantic-differential "relaxed/comfortable" ratings in forest studies and PANAS-PA. *Lonely*, *bored* and *restless* were essentially never measured alongside the others; the exceptions are UCLA loneliness in one yoga RCT (PMID 41846846) and boredom/friendliness in Morita 2007.
- Subscale tables for the forest single-session studies, Hatha yoga 2026 (PMID 42121565), the horticultural study and Zeidan 2010 were not read (some are not in PMC), so effect sizes per subscale are missing for most rows.
- Stretching as a stand-alone single-session mood intervention: no multi-dimension PubMed study surfaced. Workplace active micro-break reviews mention stretching (PMID 36292520) but report pain, fatigue and "mood" only qualitatively.
- Loving-kindness: no multi-dimension mood study (POMS/DASS) surfaced; evidence is on social connection, PA and implicit NA.
- Reed & Ones 2006, Loy 2013 and Kotera 2022 are not in PubMed, and their numbers could not be re-verified.

## 2. Meta-analyses reporting subscale-level pooled effects

### Takeaway
Subscale-level pooling exists for forest therapy (POMS, two 2025 meta-analyses), single-bout exercise (energy vs fatigue, Loy 2013), micro-breaks (vigor vs fatigue), breathwork (stress, anxiety and depression pooled separately), Baduanjin and tai chi/qigong (several outcomes in one review), and cold water. None pools outcomes within the same participants as a joint outcome. All give parallel per-dimension estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Forest therapy POMS meta-analysis: ≥60-min sessions improved all six subscales (e.g. F −2.129, V +1.996 WMD); 15–60 min left F and V ns; dynamic beat static; Asian samples showed larger effects than European samples, where F and V were ns [PM-full] — [PMID 41256089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41256089/)
- Thermal-condition forest meta-analysis: neutral conditions gave tension SMD −1.36, fatigue −0.90, confusion −1.10, vigor +1.23; inconsistent in cold or heat [PM-abs] — [PMID 41214175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41214175/)
- Loy 2013 single-bout exercise: energy Δ 0.47 vs fatigue Δ 0.03 [prior-notes] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/21641846.2013.843266)
- Puetz 2006 chronic exercise: energy and fatigue combined δ=0.37 [PM-abs] — [PMID 17073524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073524/)
- Albulescu 2022 micro-breaks: vigor d=.36, fatigue d=.35 [PM-abs] — [PMID 36044424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/)
- Fincham 2023 breathwork: stress g −0.35, anxiety −0.32, depression −0.40 [PM-abs] — [PMID 36624160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36624160/)
- Baduanjin 2026: depression, anxiety, POMS, stress and fatigue all significant (reported as mean-difference CIs) [PM-abs] — [PMID 42220380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42220380/)
- Tai chi/qigong in adolescents 2021: anxiety and depression significant; stress and mood ns [PM-abs] — [PMID 34899487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34899487/)
- Wang 2010 tai chi: stress 0.66, anxiety 0.66, depression 0.56, mood 0.45 [prior-notes] — [PMID 20492638](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20492638/)
- Cold-water 2026: depression, stress, wellbeing and mood favoured; anxiety inconsistent [PM-abs] — [PMID 42819812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42819812/)
- POMS and athletic performance meta-analysis (not an intervention): Hedges g tension −0.21, depression −0.43, anger −0.08, vigor 0.38, fatigue −0.13, confusion −0.41. Listed only to show the subscales separate in sport samples [PM-abs] — [PMID 34542449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542449/)

### Inferences
- Forest therapy is the only activity with a pooled, same-instrument estimate across all six POMS dimensions. It is the strongest available basis for "one activity → several negative emotions at once", with the caveats of heterogeneity (I² mostly 65–93%), publication bias on tension and geographic concentration in East Asia.
- The breathwork, tai chi and Baduanjin effects are similar in size across stress, anxiety and depression. This is consistent with broad action on shared distress, but because the estimates come from different trial subsets they do not prove joint change in the same people.

### Gaps
- No yoga or mindfulness meta-analysis with DASS subscales pooled separately surfaced in these PubMed searches; the queries "yoga DASS-21 meta-analysis" and "MBSR DASS-21 meta-analysis healthy" returned nothing relevant. Exercise-wide POMS-subscale meta-analyses for acute bouts (beyond Loy and Reed & Ones) were not found.

## 3. Moderation by baseline state

### Takeaway
For forest bathing, starting higher on a negative dimension predicts larger improvement: chronic stress (Morita 2007), depressive tendencies (Furuyashiki 2019) and high trait anxiety for the depression-dejection change (Song 2018). This supports giving more weight to nature when several negative scores are high. For exercise the picture is mixed: resistance training helped healthy people more than ill people for anxiety, and exercise was no better than rest in depressed samples, though people with higher depressed mood still improved.

### Cited Findings
- 498 volunteers: "the higher the stress level, the greater the effect" of shinrin-yoku on acute emotions (hostility, depression, liveliness, anxiety) [PM-abs] — [Morita 2007, PMID 17055544](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17055544/)
- 155 workers: the depressive-tendency group started higher on all negative POMS subscales and lower on vigor. After forest bathing their T-A, A-H, F and C fell to the level of the non-depressive group, and higher K6 went with larger falls in TMD and fatigue. Authors acknowledge regression to the mean as a possible explanation [PM-full] — [Furuyashiki 2019, PMID 31228960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31228960/)
- 585 young adults: high trait anxiety (n=123) gave larger reductions in POMS depression-dejection after the forest walk; trait anxiety correlated with the D change (p<0.01) [PM-abs; PM-full] — [Song 2018, PMID 30544682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30544682/)
- Green-exercise analysis: the mentally ill showed one of the largest self-esteem gains; mood gains were smallest in the young and the old [PM-abs] — [Barton & Pretty 2010, PMID 20337470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337470/)
- Nature-based mindfulness pilot RCT (N=35): lower baseline well-being → larger gains in the intervention arm, not in the outdoor-walk control [PM-abs] — [Front Psychol 2026, PMID 42851293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42851293/)
- Resistance exercise for anxiety: healthy Δ=0.50 vs physical/mental illness Δ=0.19 [PM-abs] — [Gordon 2017, PMID 28819746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28819746/)
- Acute resistance exercise vs rest: depressed mood fell in the subgroups with POMS-B ≥1 and ≥4 and in those with AGAD, AMDD or both (d up to 1.26), but rest also reduced it (d 0.27–0.39) and there was no group×time difference [PM-abs] — [PMID 40440519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440519/)
- MDD sample: exercise and quiet rest reduced tension, depression, anger and fatigue equally; only exercise raised vigor [PM-abs] — [Bartholomew 2005, PMID 16331126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16331126/)
- Women with MDD: the acute antidepressant mood effect did not depend on intensity; those with greater pre-exercise depression may have a greater BDNF response (biomarker, not mood) [PM-abs] — [Meyer 2016, PMID 27697714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27697714/)
- Single-bout exercise and state anxiety: effects moderated by health status, with floor effects because most samples start with low anxiety [prior-notes] — [Ensari 2015, PMID 25899389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25899389/)
- Chronic exercise and energy/fatigue: placebo controls may themselves raise energy and lower fatigue in older adults or people with psychological distress [PM-abs] — [Puetz 2006, PMID 17073524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073524/)
- Acute aerobic exercise and positive activated affect: participants with lower pre-exercise PAA reportedly gained more [memory — not verified this session] — Reed & Ones 2006
- PMR in workers: no anxiety or depression change because baseline levels were minimal (a floor effect) [PM-abs] — [PMID 26726829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26726829/)

### Inferences
- For combination logic, nature or forest walking is the activity whose benefit is documented to *increase* with baseline negative load: stress, depressive tendencies and trait anxiety. This favours ranking it higher when several negative classes are elevated together.
- Floor effects are the main reason single-dimension benefits fail to appear (PMR in workers; anxiety after exercise). The app should expect larger observed benefit on whichever dimensions are high at check-in, and should not promise change on dimensions that are already low.
- For people with high sad+tired scores (MDD-like), the acute evidence says rest and gentle exercise help the negative dimensions about equally, and exercise adds vigor.

### Gaps
- No moderation analysis was found for yoga, breathing, tai chi, naps or cold exposure by baseline anxiety, stress or fatigue. Regression to the mean is not ruled out in the forest moderation studies.

## 4. Studies recruiting people in a mixed state

### Takeaway
A handful of trials enrolled people defined by two elevated states, and in each the activity improved both dimensions: high stress + high depressive symptoms (breathwork/WHM, HIIT, mindfulness), depressive tendencies with fatigue (forest), mixed DASS distress (PMR in pregnancy, massage vs PMR in healthcare staff), comorbid analogue GAD+MDD (resistance exercise), and burnout or fatigue in workers (yoga). Most lack an inactive control, or found no difference between active arms.

### Cited Findings
- Midlife women with high stress **and** high depressive symptoms (n=84): WHM and slow breathing with warm showers both reduced depression (−24%), anxiety (−27%) and stress (−20%), maintained at 3 months [PM-abs] — [PMID 39606690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39606690/)
- Women with high perceived stress (n=141): mindfulness, slow breathing, WHM and HIIT all reduced depression and stress over 3 weeks, with no differences between arms [PM-abs] — [PMID 41309213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41309213/)
- Women with depression or depressive tendencies (n=31): forest bathing reduced SDS depression, POMS negative emotions and subjective fatigue vs a city walk [PM-abs] — [PMID 40277811](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40277811/)
- Workers with depressive tendencies within a general working sample (the authors frame workers as fatigued and stressed): larger multi-subscale POMS improvement [PM-full] — [PMID 31228960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31228960/)
- Pregnant women with mild-to-moderate DASS stress, anxiety and depression plus EPDS ≥10: PMR plus imagery reduced all three [PM-abs] — [PMID 29619392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29619392/)
- Healthcare professionals with moderate depression, anxiety and stress: in the massage-chair arm, depression and stress fell but anxiety did not (results for the PMR comparator were not given in the abstract) [PM-abs] — [PMID 40959182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40959182/)
- Young adults with comorbid analogue GAD+MDD: a single resistance-exercise bout reduced depressed mood, as did quiet rest [PM-abs] — [PMID 40440519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440519/)
- Men with suspected "brain fatigue" defined by POMS 2, 30-min nap-chair rest vs office-chair rest: sleepiness fell more (p=0.0004); task performance did not differ (only KSS and performance were reported) [PM-abs] — [Front Sleep 2024, PMID 41424474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41424474/)
- Blue-collar workers with fatigue, pain and burnout: workplace yoga reduced fatigue and burnout [PM-abs] — [PMID 38784274](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38784274/)

### Inferences
- The stressed+sad pair has the most direct mixed-state evidence: breathwork, mindfulness, HIIT and cold-shower protocols all helped women recruited for high stress plus depressive symptoms. Because the arms did not differ, the low-arousal options (slow breathing, mindfulness) are as good a first suggestion as the hormetic ones, and gentler.
- The sad+tired pair has direct mixed-state evidence only from forest bathing (Li 2025) and from yoga in fatigued workers.

### Gaps
- No trial recruited people who were specifically anxious *and* tired, or angry *and* stressed, and tested a single activity. These pairs remain inferential.
